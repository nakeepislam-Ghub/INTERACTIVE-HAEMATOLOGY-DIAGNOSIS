Aggressive B-cell lymphomas and Hodgkin lymphoma

Transformation of indolent lymphomas, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and its many relatives, Burkitt lymphoma, HHV8- and immune deficiency-associated proliferations, and classic and nodular lymphocyte predominant Hodgkin lymphoma.

23.1Transformation of indolent B-cell lymphomas

An aggressive lymphoma arising in a patient with a previous or synchronous, clonally related indolent B-cell lymphoma. Report it as the aggressive entity "transformed from" the indolent one (e.g. DLBCL transformed from follicular lymphoma).

  • Clues: rapidly growing nodes, worsening B symptoms, new extranodal or CNS disease; LDH >2× normal, hypercalcaemia, fast-falling counts.
  • Histology: sheets of centroblasts, immunoblasts or anaplastic cells that rarely betray the original disease.
    • CLL → Richter: DLBCL (CD5 or CD23 may persist; PD1 often positive) or classic Hodgkin lymphoma.
    • FL → follicular large B-cell lymphoma, DLBCL, or HGBL with MYC and BCL2 rearrangements (sometimes blastoid and TdT positive).
    • MZL → LBCL: sheets or clusters of more than 20 large cells; not colonised germinal centres or monocytoid nodules.
  • Prove the clonal relationship (same IG rearrangement). Prognosis is worse than de novo disease.

23.2Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, NOS

Medium to large B cells in a diffuse pattern, not meeting criteria for a specific LBCL entity. 30% of adult lymphoma; rises with age; men more. De novo, or transformed from CLL, FL, MZL or NLPHL, or in immune deficiency (HIV).

  • Morphology (it cannot be diagnosed on morphology alone): centroblastic (80%; monomorphic >90% centroblasts or polymorphic), immunoblastic (8–10%; >90% immunoblasts, often IG::MYC, worse), anaplastic (3%; CD30+, TP53), rare signet-ring or spindle forms. High mitoses, starry sky, necrosis, fibrosis.
  • IHC: CD45, pan-B (CD19, CD20, CD22, CD79a, PAX5; one may be lost). CD10, BCL6, MUM1 positive at ≥30%; BCL2 ≥50%; MYC ≥40%; Ki-67 often >80%. Cyclin D1, SOX11, CD138 and TdT negative (TdT positivity raises double hit). CD5 in a few (more in ABC).
  • Genetics: exclude MYC + BCL2 rearrangement (may be cryptic) and IRF4 rearrangement. Isolated MYC, or MYC + BCL6, stays DLBCL NOS but should be reported.

Adverse: CD5 (especially Asian patients), double expressor (MYC and BCL2 protein), high CD30, strong p53, ABC type, high IPI (calculator).

23.4Burkitt lymphoma

An aggressive germinal centre B-cell lymphoma of monomorphic medium cells with IG::MYC and a proliferation index near 100%. WHO 2022 recommends EBV-positive and EBV-negative types (replacing endemic, sporadic and immunodeficiency-associated).

  • Endemic in equatorial Africa and Papua New Guinea (malaria belt): the commonest childhood cancer there; jaw and orbit. EBV in almost all. Repeated P. falciparum infection stimulates B cells, suppresses T cells and induces AID, which creates the MYC translocation.
  • Sporadic: EBV in about 20%; abdominal (ileocaecal) masses; peaks around 10 and 60 years; M:F 2–4:1. HIV: at higher CD4 counts, often the first sign of HIV.
  • Fastest-growing human tumour: jaw and dental pain, orbit, tonsils, kidneys, testes, bilateral breasts in pregnancy or lactation. Leukaemic phase or marrow in up to 20%. High risk of tumour lysis.
  • Morphology: monomorphic medium cells, deeply basophilic vacuolated cytoplasm (lipid; adipophilin), squared-off borders, multiple small nucleoli, many mitoses and apoptoses, starry sky of tingible-body macrophages.
  • Phenotype: CD19, CD20, CD79a, PAX5; CD10 (strong), BCL6, CD38 bright, CD43, TCL1A; strong sIgM; MYC >80% of nuclei, Ki-67 >95%; BCL2 negative (weak allowed), CD44 and TdT negative. Flow: dim CD45, bright CD38 and CD81.
  • Genetics: t(8;14) IGH::MYC (or t(2;8), t(8;22) light chains); no BCL2 or BCL6 rearrangement; TP53 lesions in 25–50%.

Not Burkitt if: TdT and CD34 (lymphoblastic lymphoma), CD10 negative with strong BCL2 (HGBL NOS), MYC with BCL2 rearrangement (double hit), 11q aberration without MYC (HGBL-11q), or desmin/myogenin (rhabdomyosarcoma). Cure rates are excellent with intensive rituximab-containing regimens where resources allow.

23.6Classic Hodgkin lymphoma (CHL)

A germinal centre B-cell neoplasm in which the tumour cells (Hodgkin and Reed–Sternberg, HRS) are a small minority within a large reactive infiltrate. Peaks at 25–35 and after 60 (in white and Asian populations); infectious mononucleosis, HIV and family history raise the risk.

  • EBV in about 40%: children, the elderly, immunodeficiency, mixed cellularity and lymphocyte-depleted types; HLA-A*01 raises and HLA-A*02 lowers the risk. HRS cells show latency II (EBER, EBNA1, LMP1 mimicking CD40, LMP2A mimicking the BCR).
  • HRS cells have crippled, hypermutated IG genes and have lost the B-cell programme. They survive by NF-κB (REL gain, TNFAIP3 loss) and JAK-STAT (9p24.1 gain: JAK2, PD-L1, PD-L2) and by shaping the background: CCL17/TARC attracts Tregs; IL-5 and IL-13 bring eosinophils; M2 macrophages, CD4 T-cell rosettes and loss of MHC (B2M, CIITA) hide them from immunity.
  • Clinical: painless rubbery nodes spreading to contiguous groups; mediastinal mass (cough, SVC obstruction); B symptoms; pruritus; alcohol-induced node pain (rare, characteristic); paraneoplastic AIHA, ichthyosis, cerebellar degeneration.
  • Blood: anaemia, neutrophilia, eosinophilia, lymphopenia; high ESR/CRP.
  • Cells: classic RS cell (bi-nucleate, huge eosinophilic nucleoli, "owl's eyes"), mononuclear Hodgkin cells, lacunar cells (nodular sclerosis), mummified cells.

23.7Nodular lymphocyte predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL)

A germinal centre B-cell neoplasm of scattered large LP ("popcorn") cells with multilobed nuclei within nodules of mantle-zone B cells and follicular dendritic cells. Six growth patterns (A–F); variant patterns relapse sooner.

  • Mostly men; IgD+ cases in young men (Moraxella antigen drive; HLA-DRB1*04/*07). Rare familial cases (NPAT, TET2). EBV plays no role.
  • Peripheral nodes (cervical, axillary, inguinal); bulky or stage IV disease uncommon. Indolent but relapses.
  • LP cells are B cells: CD20, CD79a, OCT2 (most reliable), BOB1, PU.1, BCL6; CD45+; CD30 and CD15 usually negative; often IgD. Rosettes of PD1+ CD57+ TFH cells; CD21/CD23 FDC meshworks; no eosinophils.
  • Risk of transformation to LBCL (T-cell/histiocyte-rich): infradiaphragmatic and splenic disease.

23.8HIV-associated lymphomas

Non-Hodgkin lymphoma causes 23–30% of AIDS-related deaths in developed countries. Almost all are B-cell. Antiretroviral therapy has cut the overall rate, but Burkitt lymphoma has stayed level and Hodgkin lymphoma has held steady or risen.

Why HIV causes lymphoma

  • Prolonged immune suppression; EBV-driven lymphomas cluster at CD4 below 200/µL.
  • Oncogenic viruses: EBV and HHV8 (KSHV).
  • Chronic antigen stimulation, inflammation and cytokines keep B cells activated and prone to translocations.
  • HIV Tat protein switches on IL-6 and IL-10 and disturbs the pRb2/p130 tumour suppressor.

Work-up

  • CBC and film, LDH, uric acid, CD4 count, HIV viral load, hepatitis B and C, EBV viral load; HHV8 testing; immunoglobulins and electrophoresis in plasmablastic lymphoma.
  • Excision biopsy (fresh tissue for flow cytometry and DNA/RNA). IHC panel: CD3, CD5, CD10, CD20, CD30, CD45, CD138, BCL2, BCL6, MYC, MUM1, PAX5, CD15, Ki-67, κ/λ, HHV8, EBER.
  • Marrow aspirate and biopsy for staging; effusion cytology in PEL; CSF in Burkitt, neurological signs or a high CNS-IPI.
  • Tumour lysis screen before treatment: potassium, phosphate, calcium, urate, creatinine, urine output.

Outcome: with antiretroviral therapy, results approach those of HIV-negative patients. Persistently high viral load, PEL, plasmablastic and primary CNS lymphoma do worst. Score it with the HIV-IPI calculator.
