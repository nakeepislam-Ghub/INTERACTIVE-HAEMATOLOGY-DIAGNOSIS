Haematopoiesis

Where blood is made at each age, the stem cell hierarchy, and the growth factors and transcription factors that steer each lineage.

F1.1Overview

Haematopoiesis is the production of all blood cells from a small pool of self-renewing haematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). In an adult, the marrow makes roughly 2.5 × 109 red cells, 2.5 × 109 platelets and 1 × 109 granulocytes per kilogram per day, and it can raise this output several-fold under stress.

Extramedullary haematopoiesis in an adult (liver, spleen, paravertebral masses) is always abnormal. It is seen in severe chronic haemolysis, thalassaemia major and primary myelofibrosis.

F1.3The stem cell hierarchy

The classical model places the HSC at the top. It gives rise to multipotent progenitors (MPP), which split into a common myeloid progenitor (CMP) and a common lymphoid progenitor (CLP). The CMP produces the megakaryocyte–erythroid progenitor (MEP) and the granulocyte–monocyte progenitor (GMP). Single-cell studies now show this is closer to a continuum: some HSCs are already biased towards megakaryocytes, and lineage choice happens earlier than the tree suggests. The tree is still the most useful frame for interpreting immunophenotypes.

  • HSC phenotype: CD34+, CD38−, CD90+, CD45RA−, lineage-negative. CD34+ cells make up about 1% to 2% of normal marrow nucleated cells.
  • Colony assays: CFU-GEMM (mixed), BFU-E and CFU-E (erythroid), CFU-GM, CFU-Meg.
  • Niche: HSCs live close to sinusoidal endothelium and perivascular stromal cells that secrete CXCL12 (SDF-1) and stem cell factor. The CXCL12–CXCR4 axis holds them in the marrow; plerixafor blocks CXCR4 to mobilise stem cells for collection.

F1.5Transcription factors to know

GATA2 and RUNX1 maintain early progenitors. GATA1 with its cofactor FOG1, and KLF1, drive erythroid and megakaryocytic differentiation. PU.1 (SPI1) and C/EBPα drive myeloid differentiation; high PU.1 favours monocytes and lower PU.1 with C/EBPα favours granulocytes. PAX5 commits B cells, and Notch signalling with GATA3 commits T cells. Many of these genes are mutated in myeloid neoplasms (RUNX1, CEBPA, GATA2), which is why they matter later in Module 3.

F1.6Key points

  • Yolk sac (weeks 3 to 8), then liver and spleen (week 6 to month 7), then marrow from month 5
  • Adult red marrow is axial: vertebrae, pelvis, ribs, sternum, skull, proximal femur and humerus
  • HSC: CD34+ CD38− CD90+ CD45RA−, lineage-negative
  • CMP gives MEP and GMP; CLP gives B, T and NK cells
  • EPO from the kidney responds to hypoxia via HIF; TPO from the liver is constant and cleared by platelet mass
  • GATA1 and KLF1 for erythroid; PU.1 and C/EBPα for myeloid; PAX5 for B cells
