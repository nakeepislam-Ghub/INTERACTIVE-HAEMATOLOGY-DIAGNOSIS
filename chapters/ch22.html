Lymphoplasmacytic, marginal zone, follicular and mantle cell lymphoma

The small B-cell lymphomas that live in and around the lymphoid follicle: Waldenström macroglobulinaemia, the three marginal zone lymphomas, follicular lymphoma and its special types, and mantle cell lymphoma.

22.1The follicle as a map

All four are CD19/CD20-positive small B-cell lymphomas. CD5 points to mantle cell (or CLL), CD10 to follicular, and neither to marginal zone or lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma. Use the marker matcher to practise.

22.2Lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma and Waldenström macroglobulinaemia

LPL: small B lymphocytes, plasmacytoid lymphocytes and plasma cells, usually in the marrow, sometimes nodes and spleen. Waldenström macroglobulinaemia (WM) = LPL in the marrow + an IgM paraprotein. Non-IgM LPL (IgG, IgA, non-secretory, or IgM without marrow disease) has more nodal, splenic and extranodal disease and less hyperviscosity and neuropathy.

  • 2% of haematological cancers; mostly over 65; men and white people. Risks: IgM MGUS, hepatitis C, family history (20%).
  • MYD88 L265P (gain of function, NF-κB on) in >90%; CXCR4 nonsense/frameshift mutations (homing, drug resistance); del(6q).

  • Count: anaemia; leucopenia and thrombocytopenia less common. Rouleaux on the film.
  • Marrow: small lymphocytes, plasmacytoid cells, plasma cells with Dutcher bodies; increased mast cells and haemosiderin-laden macrophages; paratrabecular plus interstitial infiltrate; sometimes AL amyloid.

  • Genetics: MYD88 L265P can be found in blood, CSF (Bing–Neel), effusions and cell-free DNA.

WHO essential: marrow infiltrate >10% small lymphocytes with plasmacytoid or plasma cell differentiation; phenotype IgM+, CD19+, CD20+, CD22+, CD25+, CD10−, CD23−, CD103−, CD138 ±. Desirable: MYD88 L265P, CXCR4 mutation, IgM paraprotein on immunofixation.

22.3Marginal zone lymphomas

Small mature B cells from the marginal zone: CD5− CD10−, frequent plasmacytic differentiation, and colonised reactive follicles. WHO 2022: extranodal MZL of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT), primary cutaneous MZL (new), nodal MZL, and paediatric nodal MZL (new). Splenic MZL is in Chapter 21 and is compared here.

Gastric MALT lymphoma with H. pylori often regresses with eradication therapy, but cases with t(11;18) usually do not.

22.4Follicular lymphoma (FL)

A neoplasm of germinal centre B cells (centrocytes and centroblasts) with at least a partly follicular pattern. t(14;18)(q32;q21) IGH::BCL2 in about 85% switches on BCL2 so the cells escape the apoptosis that normally clears the germinal centre. WHO 2022 types: in situ follicular B-cell neoplasm, classic FL, follicular large B-cell lymphoma (old grade 3B), FL with uncommon features, paediatric-type FL and duodenal-type FL.

  • Grading is now optional (grades 1, 2 and 3A behave alike): centroblasts 0–15 per HPF = grade 1–2; >15 with centrocytes = 3A; sheets of centroblasts with no centrocytes = follicular large B-cell lymphoma. Diffuse areas of large cells = DLBCL.
  • Other lesions: gains of 18, X, 1q, 2p, 7; losses of 6q, 1p36 (TNFRSF14); mutations of KMT2D, CREBBP, BCL2, EZH2 (gain of function, a drug target).
  • Risks: pesticides, smoking (women), hepatitis C, Sjögren, family history.
  • Clinical: painless waxing and waning nodes, splenomegaly; B symptoms uncommon; marrow involved in 40–70%.
  • Marrow: paratrabecular aggregates (hard to aspirate, so the trephine is better). Blood cells are small cleaved ("buttock") cells.
  • Phenotype: CD19, CD20, CD22, CD79a, sIgM; germinal centre markers CD10, BCL6, GCET1, HGAL, LMO2, MEF2B; BCL2 positive (reactive follicles are BCL2 negative); MUM1, CD5, CD30 and PD1 negative; variable Ki-67.
  • FISH for BCL2, BCL6 and IRF4 when MUM1 is strong, to exclude large B-cell lymphoma with IRF4 rearrangement (children and young adults).

Prognosis: indolent. FLIPI: age >60, Hb <12 g/dL, raised LDH, Ann Arbor stage III–IV, >4 nodal areas (0–1 low, 2 intermediate, ≥3 high; calculator). Adverse genetics: TP53, 2p gain, 9p21 and 17p deletions, double-hit BCL2 and MYC. Transformation to DLBCL is suggested by a sudden LDH rise, a fast-growing node, new extranodal disease (liver, bone, muscle, brain), B symptoms, hypercalcaemia and high PET uptake.

22.5Mantle cell lymphoma (MCL)

A mature B-cell neoplasm of the follicle mantle: small to medium monomorphic cells expressing CD5, SOX11 and cyclin D1. 3–10% of B-cell lymphomas; median age 68; M:F 3–4:1. WHO 2022 groups: in situ mantle cell neoplasm (cyclin D1+ cells in the inner mantle only; rarely progresses), MCL, and leukaemic non-nodal MCL.

  • t(11;14)(q13;q32) CCND1::IGH in >90% drives cyclin D1, which overrides RB1 and p27 at the G1/S checkpoint. Cyclin D1-negative MCL uses CCND2 (or CCND3) rearrangement and is still SOX11+.
  • Secondary hits: ATM (commonest), TP53, CDKN2A, MYC, NOTCH1, KMT2D, NSD2. MYC and TP53 drive progression. IGHV mostly unmutated.
  • Clinical: nodes, splenomegaly, marrow and blood, gut (lymphomatous polyposis: diarrhoea, bleeding, weight loss). Sometimes an ALL-like or aggressive picture.
  • Blood: lymphocytosis of irregular, cleaved small to medium cells. Marrow: scattered, nodular, paratrabecular or diffuse.
  • Phenotype: pan-B, moderate-bright sIg, CD5, FMC7, CD43; CD23 and CD200 negative (the CLL contrast); CD10 and BCL6 rare (aggressive variants); cyclin D1 and SOX11 (SOX11 is also in lymphoblastic, Burkitt and hairy cell). LEF1 in blastoid cases.

WHO essential, cyclin D1-positive MCL: B-lineage cells (CD20+, usually CD5+), classic or variant morphology, cyclin D1 or CCND1 rearrangement; desirable SOX11. Cyclin D1-negative MCL: same morphology and phenotype including SOX11, no cyclin D1; desirable CCND2 rearrangement.

Differential: CLL (CD23+, CD200+, cyclin D1−), myeloma and hairy cell (can be cyclin D1+), and for blastoid or pleomorphic cases DLBCL, lymphoblastic and Burkitt lymphoma. Prognosis: considered incurable; Ki-67 >30%, strong p53 (>50% of cells), blastoid or pleomorphic morphology are adverse; isolated extranodal disease does better.

Leukaemic non-nodal MCL

  • Blood, marrow and spleen with little or no lymphadenopathy, often asymptomatic; men in their 60s. Many IGHV mutations, few genetic lesions.
  • Cells may look like CLL; flow: CD5 (variable), CD20, cyclin D1; CD23 and CD200 on a subset; low CD38; SOX11 low or negative.
  • Often managed by watch and wait; acquisition of TP53 lesions brings a fast-growing spleen, nodes and blastoid change.
