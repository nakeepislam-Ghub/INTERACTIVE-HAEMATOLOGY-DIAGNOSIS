21.1Mature B-cell neoplasms: the big picture

Clonal proliferations of mature B cells. They make up more than 80% of non-Hodgkin lymphoma; median age 66, commoner in men and in white populations. The cells range from naïve B cells that carry surface Ig but have never met antigen, to plasma cells that secrete it.

  • Indolent: slow or waxing and waning nodes, organomegaly or cytopenias over years (follicular lymphoma, CLL/SLL, splenic marginal zone lymphoma).
  • Aggressive: a fast-growing mass with B symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss), fatal in weeks if untreated (DLBCL, Burkitt).

Work-up: CBC and film or tissue biopsy, immunophenotyping, genetics; clonality by light-chain restriction or clonal IG gene rearrangement; renal and liver function, albumin, calcium, urate, LDH, β2-microglobulin; lumbar puncture if CNS disease is suspected.

WHO 2022 families: small lymphocytic proliferations (MBL, CLL/SLL), splenic lymphomas, lymphoplasmacytic, marginal zone, follicular, cutaneous follicle centre and mantle cell lymphoma, transformations, large B-cell and Burkitt lymphomas, HHV8- and immune-deficiency-associated disease, Hodgkin lymphoma and plasma cell neoplasms.

21.2Monoclonal B-cell lymphocytosis (MBL)

An asymptomatic monoclonal B-cell population with no lymphadenopathy, organomegaly or other features of a B-lymphoproliferative disorder, and a clonal B-cell count <5 × 10⁹/L.

  • Very common with age: 5% at 40–50 years, 5–25% at 65–80, 50–75% over 90.
  • Mostly silent, but may cause fatigue and immune impairment (poor vaccine responses, frequent infections).
  • Flow: κ:λ >3:1 or >25% of B cells lacking sIg. CLL type: CD5, CD23, CD43, CD200, ROR1 on >20% of clonal CD19+ cells, weak CD20, CD79b, CD81 and light chain, CD10 negative.
  • Marrow and node biopsies are not indicated. 13q14 deletion is common in the CLL type.

21.3CLL/SLL: definition and biology

  • CLL: monomorphic small mature B cells co-expressing CD5 and CD23, with ≥5 × 10⁹/L in the blood.
  • SLL: the same cells, <5 × 10⁹/L in the blood, with nodal (node >15 mm), splenic or other tissue disease.
  • The commonest adult leukaemia in fair-skinned people, rare in Asia and Latin America; rises after 70; M:F 1.5–2:1. Relatives have a 5–8-fold risk; more than 40 risk SNPs.
  • Origin: antigen-experienced CD5+ memory-like B cells. Very similar (stereotyped) B-cell receptors suggest a limited set of driving antigens; some CLL receptors bind the cell's own Ig (autonomous signalling).
  • Growth happens in proliferation centres in nodes, driven by BCR signalling with T helper cells, stroma and macrophages.
  • Lesions: del(13q) (miR-15a/16-1), del(11q) (ATM), del(17p) (TP53), trisomy 12; mutations in NOTCH1, ATM, SF3B1, TP53, BIRC3. Three methylation groups: naïve-like, intermediate and memory-like.

21.4CLL/SLL: clinical picture and complications

  • Mostly asymptomatic, found on a routine count. Nodes, splenomegaly, cytopenias (marrow infiltration or autoimmune), infections (mostly chest, from hypogammaglobulinaemia and poor cellular immunity), B symptoms.
  • Rapid, asymmetric growth of a node or mass suggests transformation.

Complications

  1. Infection.
  2. Second cancers: melanoma, sarcoma, squamous cell carcinoma; therapy-related AML.
  3. Pure red cell aplasia, AIHA and immune thrombocytopenia.
  4. Accelerated CLL: proliferation centres broader than a 20× field or confluent, Ki-67 >40% or >2.4 mitoses per centre; outcome between CLL and Richter.
  5. Prolymphocytoid progression: rising prolymphocytes (it does not become B-PLL).
  6. Richter syndrome (DLBCL) in 2–8%: TP53, NOTCH1, CDKN2A, MYC. Clonally related (unmutated IGHV) cases survive less than a year; unrelated cases behave like de novo DLBCL.
  7. Classic Hodgkin lymphoma in <1%: mostly mutated CLL, EBV-positive, clonally unrelated. Scattered Reed–Sternberg-like cells in a CLL background are not enough.

21.5CLL/SLL: laboratory diagnosis

  • Count: lymphocytosis >5 × 10⁹/L. Anaemia from infiltration, AIHA, hypersplenism, red cell aplasia, haematinic deficiency or bleeding; thrombocytopenia from infiltration or ITP.
  • Film: small lymphocytes with clumped ("soccer-ball") chromatin and scant cytoplasm, smudge cells, and prolymphocytes <15%. Up to 55% prolymphocytes: atypical CLL (trisomy 12, bright sIg, CD20, FMC7). >55%: B-prolymphocytic leukaemia.
  • Marrow: lymphocytes usually >30%. Trephine pattern interstitial, nodular, mixed or diffuse (bad prognosis); paratrabecular infiltration is not typical.
  • Flow: light-chain restriction; CD19 with CD5; bright CD23 and CD200; CD43+; dim sIgM/IgD, CD20, CD22, CD79b; FMC7 negative or weak; CD10 and cyclin D1 negative. CD38, ZAP70 and CD49d are adverse. CD20 level matters for anti-CD20 therapy.
  • Other tests: small IgM paraprotein (10%), hypogammaglobulinaemia (30% at diagnosis, 60% later), high LDH and urate.

Genetics

  • Cytogenetic changes in 80–90%: del(13q14) 50%, trisomy 12 20%, 2p gain 7%, 14q loss 4%, MYC gain 3%, t(14;18) or t(14;19) 2%, complex karyotype 2% (with TP53), del(11q), del(17p), del(6q). They are prognostic, not diagnostic.
  • Mutations (3–15%): TP53, NOTCH1, SF3B1, ATM, BIRC3, POT1, MYD88; commoner at relapse.

21.6Differential diagnosis, staging and prognosis

Reactive lymphocytosis (viruses, thyrotoxicosis, chronic inflammation, cancer, autoimmunity, post-splenectomy): less condensed chromatin, more cytoplasm, polyclonal, CD5 negative B cells. Neoplastic: ALL (blasts), other B-cell lymphomas (use the marker matcher), and T/NK disorders (CD2, CD3, CD5). Follow the lymphocytosis chart.

Adverse prognostic factors

  • Blood: lymphocyte doubling time <12 months; prolymphocytes >15%; high thymidine kinase, β2-microglobulin and LDH.
  • Marrow: diffuse infiltration; plasma cells in the trephine.
  • Phenotype: ZAP70, CD38, CD49d; high soluble CD23.
  • Genetics: unmutated IGHV; stereotyped subset #2 (IGHV3-21); del(11q); del(17p)/TP53 (resistant to fludarabine); complex karyotype; ATM, NOTCH1, SF3B1, BIRC3. Isolated del(13q) is favourable unless it is in a high proportion of cells.
  • Epigenetics: naïve-like worse than intermediate, worse than memory-like.
  • Response to treatment: the most important factor.

21.7Hairy cell leukaemia (HCL)

An indolent B-cell neoplasm of cells with abundant cytoplasm and hairy projections in blood, marrow and splenic red pulp, carrying BRAF V600E in ≥95%. 2% of leukaemias; median age 60; M:F 4:1; HLA-DRB1*11, farming, pesticides and benzene are risk factors.

  • Clinical: splenomegaly with left upper quadrant pain, marrow failure and opportunistic infections; nodes rarely. Occasionally vasculitis, bleeding, bone disease.
  • Count: pancytopenia with monocytopenia (characteristic); few circulating hairy cells.
  • Hairy cell: small to medium; oval or kidney-shaped nucleus; ground-glass chromatin; no obvious nucleolus; abundant pale blue cytoplasm with circumferential projections; sometimes rod-shaped ribosome–lamella complexes.
  • Marrow: dry tap. Trephine: interstitial or patchy (diffuse later), "fried-egg" cells with clear cytoplasm and sharp borders, reticulin fibrosis, almost no mitoses. A hypocellular marrow can mimic aplastic anaemia; immunostains settle it.
  • Cytochemistry: strong granular TRAP (weak staining is not diagnostic).
  • Phenotype: bright sIg, CD20, CD22 and CD11c; CD103, CD25, CD123, TBX21 (T-bet), CD200 bright, cyclin D1 weak; CD5 and CD10 usually negative. Annexin A1 is the most specific (only with a B-cell marker, as myeloid and T cells carry it too, so it is useless for MRD). BRAF V600E protein by IHC.
  • Genetics: IGHV mutated (>85%); several clonally related isotypes; no specific karyotype.

WHO 2022 essential: typical morphology in film/aspirate or fried-egg trephine, and strong CD20 with annexin A1 by IHC or CD20/CD11c/CD103/CD25 co-expression. Desirable: BRAF V600E; CD123, bright CD22, bright CD200, bright sIg, cyclin D1, TBX21.

Treatment: uniquely sensitive to purine analogues (cladribine, pentostatin) and interferon-α. Relapse: rituximab with chemotherapy, anti-CD22 immunotoxin, BRAF inhibitors. Persistent MRD predicts earlier relapse.

21.8The other splenic lymphomas

Splenic marginal zone lymphoma (SMZL)

  • Spleen (white pulp) with villous lymphocytes: short villi at one or two poles; a mixed population with plasmacytoid cells. Massive splenomegaly; autoimmunity in 20% (AIHA, cold agglutinins, cryoglobulins, acquired angioedema); hepatitis C link; small paraprotein in 30%.
  • Marrow: intertrabecular nodules plus intrasinusoidal infiltration (CD20 stain helps).
  • Phenotype: CD20, CD79a, PAX5, FMC7, CD27, IgM, IgD; negative CD5, CD10, CD103, annexin A1, cyclin D1, SOX11, LEF1, BCL6; CD11c sometimes.
  • Genetics: del(7q31-32) (the only specific marker), +3, +18; NOTCH2 and KLF2 mutations.
  • Median survival >10 years; worse with anaemia, low platelets, high LDH, extra-hilar nodes, NOTCH2/KLF2 mutation, transformation.

Splenic diffuse red pulp small B-cell lymphoma (SDRPL)

  • Diffuse red pulp infiltration by monomorphic villous cells with broad-based, polar projections (1–4 poles). Massive spleen, few B symptoms, moderate lymphocytosis, no monocytopenia.
  • Marrow: intrasinusoidal. Phenotype: IgG, DBA44, cyclin D3 (70%); negative CD5, CD23, CD25, CD103 (mostly), annexin A1. CD200/CD180 MFI ratio <0.5 favours SDRPL. No BRAF, NOTCH2 or KLF2 mutations.

Splenic B-cell lymphoma/leukaemia with prominent nucleoli (formerly HCL variant)

  • Median age 71. High lymphocytosis, no monocytopenia; cells with a single large nucleolus and fine, non-circumferential projections.
  • CD11c, CD103 and FMC7 positive, but CD25, annexin A1, TRAP and CD123 negative; no BRAF V600E (MAP2K1 mutations instead).
  • More aggressive than HCL; no response to interferon, partial response to cladribine.
