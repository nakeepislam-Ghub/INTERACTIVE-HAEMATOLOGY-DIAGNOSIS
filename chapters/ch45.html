Erythropoiesis and red cell physiology

The stages from proerythroblast to red cell, haemoglobin and globin switching, oxygen delivery, red cell metabolism, the membrane and lifespan.

F2.1Overview

Committed erythroid progenitors (BFU-E, then CFU-E) give rise to the first morphologically recognisable cell, the proerythroblast. Through about four divisions over 5 to 7 days, each proerythroblast yields around 16 red cells. Erythroblasts mature in erythroblastic islands around a central macrophage, which supplies iron and engulfs the extruded nuclei.

Reticulocytes spend about 1 to 2 days maturing in the marrow and about 1 day in the blood. Under strong EPO drive they are released early ("shift" or stress reticulocytes) and stay longer in the circulation, which is why the reticulocyte production index corrects for this.

F2.3Haemoglobin

Each haemoglobin molecule is a tetramer of two α-like and two β-like globin chains, each carrying one haem group (protoporphyrin IX with ferrous iron). The α-globin cluster is on chromosome 16 (two α genes per chromosome, so four in total) and the β-globin cluster is on chromosome 11.

F2.4Oxygen delivery

The oxygen dissociation curve is sigmoid because binding is cooperative. The P50 (oxygen tension at 50% saturation) is about 26.6 mmHg (3.5 kPa) in adult blood. The curve shifts right, releasing more oxygen to tissues, with acidosis (the Bohr effect), raised CO2, fever and raised 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG). HbF binds 2,3-BPG poorly, so fetal blood has a left-shifted curve (P50 about 19 mmHg), which favours oxygen transfer across the placenta. Try the interactive curve in the Animations section.

F2.5Red cell metabolism

  • Glycolysis (Embden–Meyerhof pathway): about 90% of glucose. Produces ATP for the cation pumps and membrane, and NADH for cytochrome b5 reductase, which keeps haem iron in the ferrous state. Pyruvate kinase deficiency is the commonest defect here.
  • Hexose monophosphate shunt: about 10% of glucose. G6PD produces NADPH, which regenerates reduced glutathione to protect against oxidant damage.
  • Rapoport–Luebering shunt: makes 2,3-BPG, which lowers oxygen affinity.

F2.6Membrane and lifespan

The membrane skeleton is a spectrin lattice tied to the lipid bilayer vertically (via ankyrin, band 3 and protein 4.2) and horizontally (via protein 4.1, actin and adducin). Vertical defects cause hereditary spherocytosis; horizontal defects cause elliptocytosis. Red cells survive about 120 days and are removed mainly by splenic and hepatic macrophages. Haem is converted to biliverdin and then unconjugated bilirubin, and the iron is recycled through ferroportin under the control of hepcidin.

F2.7Key points

  • Proerythroblast → basophilic → polychromatic → orthochromatic erythroblast → reticulocyte → red cell
  • The polychromatic erythroblast is the last stage that divides
  • One proerythroblast yields about 16 red cells over 5 to 7 days
  • HbA about 97%, HbA2 2.2–3.5%, HbF under 1% in adults
  • P50 about 26.6 mmHg; right shift with acidosis, CO2, fever and 2,3-BPG
  • Glycolysis gives ATP and NADH; the HMP shunt gives NADPH via G6PD
  • Red cell lifespan about 120 days
