29.1History and examination

  • Personal: age (senile purpura in the old, congenital disease in the young); sex (X-linked haemophilia in males).
  • Past and drug history: liver or renal disease, malignancy, diet, antibiotics, anticoagulants, antiplatelets.
  • Menstrual and family history.
  • Bleeding history: frequency, provoked or not, type (petechiae <1 mm, purpura 1 mm–1 cm, ecchymoses >1 cm) and site.

29.2Pre-analytic variables

Key pointMost "abnormal" coagulation results with no clinical bleeding are pre-analytic errors. Get the tube right before you interpret the number.
The patient
  • Stress, exercise and illness raise fibrinogen, FVIII, VWF, D-dimer and platelet aggregation.
  • Time of day: sample between 7 and 9 am. Fibrinogen, PAI-1, FVIII, protein C and S are higher early in the morning; tPA is lowest.
  • Factor concentrates: measure FVIII 15 minutes and FIX 30 minutes after the dose.
  • Drugs: anticoagulants affect lupus anticoagulant, APC resistance, AT, protein C and S tests. Stop antiplatelets 7–10 days before platelet function tests.
  • Pregnancy raises VII, X, fibrinogen, VIII, VWF, PAI-1, PAI-2 and D-dimer, and lowers free protein S. Oral contraceptives affect protein S and APC resistance.
  • No smoking for 2 hours; avoid fatty food; sit relaxed for 20–30 minutes.
  • Testing too soon after thrombosis or on anticoagulants gives false "deficiencies" and a raised FVIII that can hide a lupus anticoagulant.
Collection
  • Peripheral vein away from any drip; atraumatic; discard the first 1–2 mL.
  • A long tourniquet causes haemoconcentration and raises fibrinogen and factors VII, VIII and XII.
  • Plastic or siliconised glass. In an evacuated system the citrate tube is the second or third tube, to avoid tissue factor.
  • Trisodium citrate 3.2% (0.109 M), 1 part to 9 parts blood; invert gently.
Processing, storage and rejection
  • Keep at room temperature: cold activates platelets and factor VII. Transport within 1 hour (V and VIII are labile).
  • PT samples are stable 24 hours; heparin samples must be processed within 1 hour (PF4 release neutralises heparin); APTT and special assays within 4 hours, or separate and freeze.
  • Platelet-rich plasma: 180 g for 10 min. Platelet-poor plasma: 2000 g for 15 min; double-spin to get platelets <10 × 10⁹/L.
  • Haemolysis and lipaemia interfere with optical detection; haemolysis releases tissue factor.
  • Reject: unidentified, older than 4 hours, clotted, wrong anticoagulant, wrong citrate-to-blood ratio.

29.4Vascular and platelet tests

Bleeding time

  • Duke: 3 mm ear-lobe incision; normal 1–4 min. Ivy: cuff at 40 mmHg, template forearm incisions; normal 2.5–10 min.
  • Prolonged in thrombocytopenia, thrombasthenia, vascular disorders, severe hypofibrinogenaemia and VWD.
  • Hess test (obsolete): cuff at 100 mmHg for 5 min; more than 5 petechiae in 5 cm is positive.

Platelet count, film and indices

The film excludes pseudothrombocytopenia and shows primary platelet disease (giant or grey platelets, Döhle-like bodies). MPV and PDW are low in hypoproduction and high in peripheral destruction.

PFA-100

Citrated whole blood flows at high shear through a collagen/epinephrine (CEPI, 78–199 s) or collagen/ADP (CADP, 55–137 s) membrane until platelets close it. Fast and automated, with a high negative predictive value, except for storage pool disease, secretion defects and mild type 1 VWD.

Light transmission aggregometry

  • Fast 12–24 h, no drugs for 10 days, avoid onion and garlic. Normal platelet count; process within 4 h at room temperature.
  • PRP is adjusted to 200–400 × 10⁹/L; PPP sets 100% transmission, PRP 0%. 270 µL PRP + 30 µL agonist at 37 °C, stirred at 900 rpm.
  • Latent period: short with ristocetin, medium with high-dose ADP, long with collagen. Maximal aggregation normally 40–60%.
  • Low-dose ADP gives a primary wave, then a secondary wave from TXA₂ and granule ADP. Ristocetin causes agglutination through VWF and GPIb.
Other platelet tests
  • Clot retraction: normal 40–60%; reduced in thrombocytopenia and thrombasthenia.
  • Granule content: electron microscopy, lumiaggregometry (ATP release), β-TG, PF4.
  • Platelet coagulant activity: prothrombin consumption index (<30%); Stypven time with PRP.
  • Flow cytometry: CD41, CD61, CD42 (GPIIb, IIIa, Ib), CD62P and CD63 for activation.
  • Platelet survival: ⁵¹Cr labelling, or the return of cyclo-oxygenase activity after one dose of aspirin.

Mixing studies and factor assays

A 1:1 mix with normal plasma separates a deficiency (corrects) from an inhibitor (does not). Repeating after 2 hours at 37 °C picks up time-dependent inhibitors such as acquired anti-VIII. Adsorbed plasma (lacks II, VII, IX, X) and aged plasma (lacks V, VIII) narrow down the factor. Walk through it in the mixing study simulator.

Factor assays use a parallel-line bioassay: serial dilutions of patient and standard plasma are mixed with factor-deficient plasma. A patient curve to the left of the standard means less factor activity. Immunoassays measure the amount, not the function.

29.6Fibrinolysis and factor XIII

  • FDPs (normal <10 µg/mL): raised in DIC, streptokinase therapy, thrombosis, MI, after major surgery.
  • D-dimer: from cross-linked fibrin; an excellent negative test. False positives in old age, haemolysis, rheumatoid factor, lipaemia, jaundice.
  • Euglobulin lysis time (normal 90–240 min): short in FXIII deficiency; long after MI, surgery, thrombosis, in obesity and late pregnancy.
  • Fibrin plate lysis (40–60 mm²) and the venous occlusion test (lysis should shorten by 30 min after 10 min of cuff occlusion).
  • Factor XIII: PT and APTT are normal. In the clot solubility test, a clot without FXIII dissolves in 5 M urea within 24 hours.

29.7Viscoelastic tests: TEG and ROTEM

Thromboelastography follows a whole-blood clot from the first fibrin strand to its lysis, so it shows factors, platelets, fibrinogen and fibrinolysis in one tracing. It guides transfusion in cardiac surgery, liver transplantation, trauma and major obstetric haemorrhage.

A cup of blood (alone, or with activators, heparinase, antifibrinolytic or protamine) oscillates around a pin. As the clot grows it couples the pin to the cup, and a transducer records the tension.

  • Hypercoagulability: short R and K, big angle and MA.

Other versions: ROTEM (rotational thromboelastometry; the pin rotates in 300 µL of citrated blood and is read optically), Sonoclot (viscosity change before fibrin polymerises), and platelet mapping, which measures the percentage inhibition by aspirin or P2Y12 drugs from MAthrombin, MAfibrin and MAADP or AA:

Platelet inhibition (%) = 100 − [(MAADP or AA − MAfibrin) ÷ (MAthrombin − MAfibrin) × 100]
