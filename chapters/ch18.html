Acute leukaemias of mixed or ambiguous lineage

When blasts will not pick a side: lineage-assignment rules, mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia with BCR::ABL1, KMT2A, ZNF384 or BCL11B, the immunophenotypic types, and acute undifferentiated leukaemia.

18.1Definition and subtypes

Leukaemias with at least 20% abnormal progenitors that do not differentiate along a single lineage. Rare: under 4% of acute leukaemia, in children and adults.

  • Acute undifferentiated leukaemia (AUL): no clear lineage commitment.
  • Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL): differentiation along more than one lineage, either biphenotypic (one blast population carrying antigens of two or more lineages) or bilineal (two blast populations, each of a different lineage).

The phenotype can change over time or with treatment (lineage switch), especially with KMT2A rearrangement, ZNF384 fusions, DUX4 rearrangement and PAX5 P80R.

Excluded from MPALTherapy-related acute leukaemia, blast phase of CML, and leukaemias with another defining recurrent genetic change are classified by that change, even when the immunophenotype looks mixed. Only BCR::ABL1 and KMT2A rearrangement define MPAL types.

18.2Assigning lineage

Flow cytometry is the method of choice. Immunohistochemistry can be used, but separating leukaemic from normal cells is harder. Cytochemical MPO can support myeloid lineage.

  • CD79a is often expressed in T-ALL/LBL, so it is not counted for B lineage when T lineage is being considered. B lineage without CD19 is rare, but three strong B antigens can establish it.
  • Polyclonal CD3 antibodies on IHC can react with the CD3 ζ chain in NK cells.
  • When MPO is dim, its specificity improves if the MPO+ cells also have higher light scatter, CD117, or bright CD13/CD33 that differ from the lymphoid-looking blasts. If MPO is dim or negative, monocytic differentiation by flow or diffuse NSE is enough.

18.5Immuno­phenotypically defined types

  • MPAL, B/myeloid: adults and children; ZNF384 in children, PAX5 and IKZF1, RAS pathway (NRAS, PTPN11) and epigenetic mutations. Usually an abnormal karyotype (complex, +4, del(6q), chromosome 7 changes, near-tetraploidy). Outcome between AML and B-ALL.
  • MPAL, T/myeloid: the second commonest (20–30% after excluding BCL11B). PICALM::MLLT10 in 10–15% (often cryptic); WT1 with ETV6 and RUNX1 in children; DNMT3A and IDH2 in adults; PHF6, FLT3. Some express monocytic markers. High risk.
  • Rare types: B/T (about 6%) and B/T/myeloid (about 3%) in adolescents and young adults (median age 15); T/megakaryocytic in infants, with non-constitutional +21 and CBFA2T3::GLIS2. PHF6, NOTCH1, JAK3, IL7R, RUNX1 mutations. Poor survival.
  • ALAL, NOS: marker combinations that allow no definite lineage and do not fit AUL or MPAL; full genetics needed first. Poor prognosis.
  • Acute undifferentiated leukaemia: 1–2% of acute leukaemia, adults, male to female 1.5 : 1. Blasts carry stem cell markers but lack cCD3, MPO, cCD22, CD79a, strong CD19 and NK, megakaryocyte, basophil and pDC markers; MPO and SBB negative. Abnormal karyotype in 40%; PHF6, RUNX1, SRSF2, ASXL1, BCOR; SET::NUP214. Differential: AML with minimal differentiation, rare MPAL, non-haemopoietic tumours.
