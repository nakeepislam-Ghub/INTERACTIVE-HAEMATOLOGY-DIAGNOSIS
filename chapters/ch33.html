Thrombophilia

Inherited and acquired tendencies to thrombosis: factor V Leiden, the prothrombin variant, antithrombin, protein C and S deficiency, hyperhomocysteinaemia and antiphospholipid syndrome.

33.1Causes

InheritedAcquired
Loss of anticoagulants (type I): antithrombin, protein C, protein S, HCII, protein Z deficiency; fibrinolytic defects.
Gain of procoagulants (type II, commoner): factor V Leiden (commonest), prothrombin G20210A, high fibrinogen, VIII, VII; dysfibrinogenaemia.
Others: homocystinuria, sickle cell disease.		 Antiphospholipid syndrome; oestrogen (pill, HRT); pregnancy; immobility; surgery and catheters; malignancy (APL, MPN); chemotherapy (L-asparaginase, thalidomide, tamoxifen); HIT; PNH; TTP; inflammation; acquired hyperhomocysteinaemia.

When to suspect it

  • Thrombosis before 40, recurrent or unprovoked, or at unusual sites (cerebral, mesenteric, portal veins).
  • Strong family history; neonatal purpura fulminans; warfarin skin necrosis; neonatal stroke.
  • Recurrent pregnancy loss, IUGR, severe pre-eclampsia, abruption.

33.2Laboratory approach

No single test detects every thrombophilia.

  • Screen: CBC (polycythaemia, thrombocytosis), PT, APTT (short in FV Leiden; long with LA), TT, fibrinogen.
  • Activation markers: D-dimer, F1+2, TAT, fibrinopeptide A.
  • Heritable: APC resistance and FV Leiden PCR; prothrombin G20210A PCR; functional antithrombin, protein C, free protein S.
  • Acquired: LA and solid-phase antibodies; CD55/CD59 flow for PNH; JAK2 for MPN; homocysteine.
Timing mattersDo not measure antithrombin, protein C or S during acute thrombosis or on heparin or warfarin. Protein S falls in pregnancy and on the pill.

33.4Antiphospholipid syndrome

An autoimmune disorder of recurrent thrombosis and pregnancy morbidity caused by antiphospholipid antibodies (primary, or secondary to SLE, infection, drugs, malignancy). Antibodies against β2-glycoprotein I activate platelets, endothelium, monocytes (tissue factor) and complement, and block annexin A5, protein C and fibrinolysis. A "second hit" such as infection or surgery often triggers clotting.

Sydney criteria: at least one clinical and one laboratory criterion

ClinicalLaboratory (on 2 occasions ≥12 weeks apart)
Arterial, venous or small-vessel thrombosis.
Pregnancy: ≥3 unexplained miscarriages before 10 weeks; ≥1 fetal death after 10 weeks; or premature birth before 34 weeks from pre-eclampsia or placental insufficiency.		 Lupus anticoagulant (ISTH guidelines).
Anticardiolipin IgG/IgM, medium or high titre.
Anti-β2GPI IgG/IgM, medium or high titre.

Lupus anticoagulant testing

  1. Sample before anticoagulation; double-centrifuge to platelets <10 × 10⁹/L; freeze quickly; thaw at 37 °C.
  2. Use two tests based on different principles: DRVVT first, then a sensitive APTT (low phospholipid, silica activator).
  3. Screen prolonged → mix (no correction) → confirm (shortens with excess phospholipid).
  4. Positive if screen/confirm ratio >1.2, or percentage correction >10%. Try the calculator.

Treatment: warfarin (INR 2–3, or 3–4 after arterial events or recurrence). DOACs are avoided in triple-positive patients. In pregnancy, LMWH plus low-dose aspirin. Catastrophic APS: anticoagulation, steroids, plasma exchange, IVIg.

33.5Acquired settings: immobility, pregnancy and cancer

SettingKey points
ImmobilityAfter surgery, heart failure or MI, varicose veins, atrial fibrillation, muscle relaxants, long flights. Activated factors accumulate where blood stagnates. Prevent with hydration, calf exercise and early walking.
Pregnancy90% are lower-limb DVTs, mostly iliofemoral and on the left (the right iliac artery crosses the left iliac vein); isolated calf DVT is uncommon. Raised VIII and fibrinogen from week 10, falling protein S, less fibrinolysis, venous compression. Worse with obesity, inherited thrombophilia, trauma and caesarean delivery.
Solid cancerMost often ovary, brain and pancreas; also lymphoma, leukaemia, colon and lung. Trousseau syndrome (migratory thrombophlebitis), chronic DIC with mucin-secreting adenocarcinoma, APS with paraproteins. Stasis, vessel invasion and catheters, tumour tissue factor and cancer procoagulant, cytokines, chemotherapy. LMWH is preferred.
Polycythaemia veraVenous and arterial thrombosis, linked to haematocrit, age, previous thrombosis and vascular risk factors; the platelet count is not a risk factor.
Essential thrombocythaemiaMainly arterial; erythromelalgia, livedo, cerebral ischaemia from platelet changes.
Acute promyelocytic leukaemiaTissue factor and cancer procoagulant on promyelocytes, leucostasis made worse by ATRA (Chapter 31).
Note on the source: one line of the notes lists protein S as rising in pregnancy; free protein S actually falls, which is why protein S cannot be assessed reliably during pregnancy.
