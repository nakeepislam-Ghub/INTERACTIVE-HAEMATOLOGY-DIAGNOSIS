The automated blood count

How analysers count and size cells by light scatter and impedance, where spurious results come from, and how to read red cell, white cell and platelet histograms, their flags, and the white cell scattergram in health and disease.

40.1What the analyser measures

The automated blood picture is a series of tests of the composition and concentration of the cells in blood:

  • Red cells: count, haemoglobin concentration and indices (MCV, MCH, MCHC, RDW).
  • White cells: total and differential count.
  • Platelets: count and indices (MPV, PDW).

40.2How it works

Light scattering

  • Diluted blood is injected into a sheath stream and hydrodynamically focused so cells pass a laser beam in single file, one at a time.
  • Each cell scatters light onto photodetectors: forward scatter measures size and side (right-angle) scatter measures granularity. Each signal becomes an electrical pulse.
  • The number of pulses in a set time is proportional to the number of cells.

Electrical impedance

  • Cells do not conduct electricity; the diluent does. Cells are drawn through an aperture between two electrodes and each one briefly raises the impedance.
  • The number of pulses gives the count; the height of each pulse gives the cell's volume. The computer sorts the pulses into histograms (size on the x axis, number on the y axis).

The two baths

BathWhat happens
Red cell and platelet bathFurther dilution. Red cells and platelets are counted in the same aperture; platelets are the particles of 2–30 fL. White cells are present but too few to matter, unless the count is very high (for example CLL), when they distort the red cell histogram.
White cell and haemoglobin bathA lysing agent destroys red cells and shrinks white cells to a size set by their nucleus. Monocytes (about 20 µm) shrink most, and with eosinophils and basophils form one mixed middle population; lymphocytes and neutrophils shrink least. The lyse contains potassium cyanide, which turns haemoglobin into cyanmethaemoglobin, whose optical density is proportional to the haemoglobin concentration.

40.4Histograms

A histogram shows how a set of measurements is distributed: the x axis is the value (cell volume), the y axis how often it occurs. Explore every pattern below in the histogram explorer.

Red cell histogram

  • A symmetrical bell curve. Macrocytosis shifts it right, microcytosis left.
  • The curve should start and end on the baseline between the discriminators: lower discriminator 25–75 fL, upper 200–250 fL. Particles of 25–250 fL are counted; the peak area (60–125 fL) gives MCV and RDW.
  • RDW-SD is the width of the curve at 20% of its height, in fL (normal 35–45 fL). RDW-CV = SD ÷ MCV × 100 (normal 11.5–14.5%).

White cell histogram (3-part differential)

  • Lower discriminator 30–60 fL, upper fixed at 300 fL; the cells between them are the WBC count.
  • Two troughs, T1 (78–114 fL) and T2 (under 150 fL), give three groups: small cells 35–90 fL (lymphocytes), mid cells 90–160 fL (monocytes, eosinophils, basophils, promyelocytes), large cells 160–300 fL (neutrophils).

Platelet histogram

  • A single right-skewed peak. MPV is the average platelet size (7.4–10.4 fL); PDW is the width of the size distribution.
  • PL flag (lower discriminator over 10%): high blank, dust or bubbles, electrical noise, red cell or white cell fragments, bacteria.
  • PU flag (upper discriminator over 40%): platelet clumps, giant platelets, severe microcytosis, fragmented red cells, leucocyte fragments.
  • MP flag (several peaks): platelet anisocytosis, recovery after chemotherapy, clumps.

40.5Scattergrams

A scattergram plots two measurements against each other; each cell is one dot, placed where its two channel values meet.

Abbott Cell-Dyn (Ruby)

  • Mononuclear vs polymorphonuclear: 90° against 10° scatter. Mononuclear cells have lower values of both.
  • Neutrophil vs eosinophil: 90° depolarised against 90° scatter, for polymorphs only. Eosinophil granules depolarise light, so eosinophils sit higher.
  • Mononuclear separation: 0° against 10° scatter. Basophil granules dissolve in the lyse, so basophils fall among mononuclear cells: lymphocytes lowest, basophils slightly higher, monocytes highest.

Sysmex 5-part analysers

  • Combine impedance and radiofrequency conductance, a diode laser with side scatter (SSC) and forward scatter (FSC), and a polymethine dye that stains RNA and DNA for side fluorescence (SFL).
  • In the WDF channel cells are sorted by SSC and SFL into lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and neutrophils plus basophils. Basophils are counted separately in the WBC/BASO channel, whose lyse spares basophil granules.
  • Neutrophils (violet): central dense teardrop, spreading upwards with immature forms. Lymphocytes (blue): thumbprint at the bottom left. Monocytes (green): circular cluster above the lymphocytes. Eosinophils (pink): irregular cluster at the right. Basophils (white): small cluster in the lower left of the neutrophil area.
