Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasms

The overlap neoplasms: high counts in one lineage, cytopenia and dysplasia in another. CMML, MDS/MPN with neutrophilia, MDS/MPN with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis, and MDS/MPN not otherwise specified.

Source: Dr Abeer's Myeloid Proliferations and Neoplasms notes, pp 169–191Reading time about 30 min

16.1Definition and the WHO 2022 types

Myeloid neoplasms with overlapping pathological and molecular features of myelodysplastic and myeloproliferative neoplasms.

  • Hypercellular marrow from increased proliferation of one or more myeloid lineages, and raised blood counts because that proliferation is effective.
  • Dysplasia, morphological or functional, and cytopenia in other lineages because their production is ineffective.
  • Not included: a well-defined MPN that later develops dysplasia (that is progression), and cases with BCR::ABL1, rearranged PDGFRA, PDGFRB or FGFR1, or PCM1::JAK2.
  1. Chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia (CMML)
  2. MDS/MPN with neutrophilia
  3. MDS/MPN with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis
  4. MDS/MPN, not otherwise specified

16.2Chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia: the disease

A myeloid neoplasm with sustained blood monocytosis and combinations of somatic mutations in epigenetic regulators, the spliceosome and signal transduction.

  • Who: median age 71–76; male to female 1.6–2.6 : 1. Arises from age-related clonal haemopoiesis; some cases have a familial germline predisposition.
  • Pathogenesis: mutations in DNA methylation (TET2), histone modification (ASXL1), splicing (SRSF2, U2AF1), signalling (NRAS, KRAS, CBL, JAK2) and transcription factors (SETBP1, RUNX1). Hypersensitivity to GM-CSF, abnormal cytokines, and deficient IL-10 drive myelomonocytic expansion.
  • Clinical: hepatosplenomegaly and constitutional symptoms (weight loss, fever, sweats) with the proliferative type; fatigue, infection and bleeding with the dysplastic type. Lymphadenopathy is uncommon and may mean transformation.

16.3CMML: laboratory diagnosis

Blood

  • Persistent absolute (≥0.5 × 10⁹/L) and relative (≥10%) monocytosis, usually 2–5 × 10⁹/L, sometimes over 80 × 10⁹/L. Life-threatening hyperleucocytosis can occur.
  • Monocytes are mostly mature; abnormal forms have denser chromatin, more nuclear folds and more greyish cytoplasm than promonocytes. Blasts + promonocytes <20%.
  • Neutrophilia is commoner than neutropenia; promyelocytes and myelocytes usually <10%; dysgranulopoiesis (less in the proliferative type); mild basophilia; eosinophils normal unless CMML with eosinophilia (>1.5 × 10⁹/L).
  • Anaemia, normocytic or macrocytic; thrombocytopenia with large platelets (sometimes thrombocytosis).

Marrow

  • Usually hypercellular with granulocytic and monocytic hyperplasia and left shift; blasts + promonocytes counted as above.
  • Dysgranulopoiesis; relative fall in erythropoiesis with mild dyserythropoiesis; megakaryocytes increased but not clustered, with micromegakaryocytes and hypolobation.
  • Nodules of mature clonal plasmacytoid dendritic cells (round nuclei, fine chromatin, rim of eosinophilic cytoplasm) in about 20%; CD123+.
  • No pseudo-Gaucher cells. Mild reticulin fibrosis; more fibrosis in proliferative cases with JAK2 V617F and splenomegaly.

Special tests

  • Cytochemistry: monocytes are positive for fluoride-sensitive α-naphthyl butyrate esterase, α-naphthyl acetate esterase and lysozyme. A double esterase stain separates monocytes from granulocytes.
  • Flow cytometry: CD13 and CD33 positive; increased classical monocytes (CD14+CD16−) above 94%; aberrant CD56, CD2; loss of HLA-DR, CD13, CD11c, CD15, CD16, CD64, CD36. Aberrant CD34+ blasts (bright CD13, CD34, CD117, CD123; dim CD38; CD7, CD56) point to early transformation.
  • Immunohistochemistry: less sensitive; CD14 is the most reliable, with CD68R and CD163. Plasmacytoid dendritic cells: CD123, CD4, CD43, CD303, granzyme B; low Ki-67.
  • Spleen red pulp and lymph node infiltration may occur; polyclonal hypergammaglobulinaemia is common.
  • Genetics: no BCR::ABL1 and no PDGFRA, PDGFRB, FGFR1 or PCM1::JAK2. Clonal karyotype in 23–34%: +8, −7/del(7q), −Y, +12; complex karyotype is uncommon.

With an active autoimmune disease or systemic inflammatory syndrome, a classical monocyte fraction above 94% is not counted.

Differential diagnosis
  • CML with p190 BCR::ABL1 (monocytosis, but BCR::ABL1 positive).
  • CMML after cytotoxic therapy: classify as myeloid neoplasm post cytotoxic therapy (Chapter 19).
  • AML with NPM1 mutation, myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia and TK rearrangement, and systemic mastocytosis (classified by their own criteria).
  • An MPN that evolves to look like CMML is progression, not CMML. An MDS that later meets CMML criteria may be reclassified as CMML.

Prognosis: median survival 2–3 years; AML in 15–30%. Adverse: cytopenias; leucocytosis, monocytosis and lymphocytosis; circulating blasts or immature myeloid cells; +8, chromosome 7 changes or complex karyotype; ASXL1, RUNX1, NRAS, SETBP1 mutations; splenomegaly; extramedullary disease; high LDH, β2-microglobulin and thymidine kinase.

16.5MDS/MPN with neutrophilia

Formerly atypical CML. Sustained neutrophilia with a left shift and marked dysgranulopoiesis. Uncommon; median age in the 70s; male to female 1 : 1.

  • Pathogenesis: SETBP1 mutations destroy a degron and stabilise the protein; ETNK1 mutations deplete phosphoethanolamine, raising reactive oxygen species and mutagenesis; RAS pathway mutations (KRAS, NRAS, CBL).
  • Clinical: hepatosplenomegaly, fatigue and bleeding.
  • Blood: WBC ≥13 × 10⁹/L with ≥10% immature granulocytes (promyelocytes, myelocytes, metamyelocytes); acquired Pelger–Huët, hypersegmentation, abnormal chromatin clumping and hypogranularity; monocytes <10%; mild basophilia; anaemia and thrombocytopenia.
  • Marrow: hypercellular, M:E >10 : 1, dysgranulopoiesis, blasts <20% without sheets; dyserythropoiesis; small hypolobated megakaryocytes and micromegakaryocytes; mild fibrosis.
  • Cytochemistry: MPO may be lost; esterase stains exclude a monocytic component (CMML); NAP variable.
  • Genetics: abnormal karyotype in 30–40% (+8, del(20q), i(17q)); SETBP1 and ETNK1; also ASXL1, TET2, NRAS, CBL and others. No JAK2, CALR, MPL or CSF3R mutation (desirable criterion).
WHO 2022Criteria
EssentialLeucocytosis ≥13 × 10⁹/L with neutrophilia and ≥10% circulating immature myeloid cells, and neutrophil dysplasia; hypercellular marrow with granulocytic predominance and dysplasia; <20% blasts; not an MPN, M/LN-eo, CMML or MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T.
DesirableSETBP1 and/or ETNK1 mutation; no JAK2, CALR, MPL or CSF3R mutation.

Differential: chronic neutrophilic leukaemia (CSF3R mutation, mature neutrophils, no dysplasia), CMML, MDS/MPN-NOS. Prognosis: poor, median survival 14–29 months; worse with age >65, female sex, WBC >50, thrombocytopenia and Hb <10 g/dL.

16.6MDS/MPN with SF3B1 mutation and thrombocytosis

Formerly MDS/MPN-RS-T. SF3B1 mutation, ring sideroblasts and thrombocytosis. Median age 68–75, slight female excess.

  • Pathogenesis: mutant SF3B1 mis-splices the mitochondrial iron–sulphur transporter ABCB7, so iron piles up in mitochondria (ring sideroblasts); a co-operating JAK2 V617F, CALR or MPL mutation drives the thrombocytosis.
  • Clinical: splenomegaly in 40%; thrombotic risk higher than ET.
  • Blood: normocytic anaemia with a dimorphic picture; WBC usually normal; platelets ≥450 × 10⁹/L from tiny to giant; no blasts.
  • Marrow: hypercellular with erythroid hyperplasia, megaloblastoid change, ≥15% ring sideroblasts, and large MPN-like megakaryocytes (as in ET or PMF); fibrosis in some.
  • Genetics: abnormal karyotype in 15%; must have no BCR::ABL1, no TK rearrangement, no del(5q), no 3q26 (MECOM) change.

16.7MDS/MPN, not otherwise specified

Dysplastic and proliferative features that fit no other MDS/MPN type. The second commonest overlap syndrome; median age about 70; men >60%.

  • Clinical: weight loss, sweats, splenomegaly, thrombosis, fatigue, dyspnoea, infection, bleeding; sometimes transfusion-dependent anaemia.
  • Laboratory: anaemia, then thrombocytopenia (neutropenia rare), with platelets ≥450 or WBC ≥13 × 10⁹/L; high LDH; marrow fibrosis in 30%.
  • Genetics: abnormal karyotype in about 50% (+8, −7/del(7q), del(20q), complex); TET2, NRAS, RUNX1, CBL, SETBP1, ASXL1, TP53. An SF3B1 mutation should prompt careful exclusion of MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T.
  • Exclude: therapy-related neoplasm; BCR::ABL1 and PDGFRA, PDGFRB, FGFR1, JAK2 rearrangement; double-hit TP53; CMML, MDS/MPN-N and MDS/MPN-SF3B1-T.
  • Special situation: MDS that gains new proliferative features may be called MDS/MPN-NOS once reactive causes (growth factors, other therapy) are excluded.
  • Treatment: as for MDS or MPN, guided by symptoms and cytopenias; growth factors for cytopenias, cytoreduction for leucocytosis. Prognosis is variable.
