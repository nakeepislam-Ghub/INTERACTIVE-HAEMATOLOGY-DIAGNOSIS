Mastocytosis

Clonal mast cell disease driven by KIT: cutaneous and systemic forms, B and C findings, the WHO criteria, the subtypes from indolent disease to mast cell leukaemia, and how the laboratory proves it.

14.1What mastocytosis is

A rare clonal neoplastic proliferation and accumulation of abnormal mast cells in one or more organs.

  • Cutaneous mastocytosis is commonest in children and may be present at birth; slight male predominance.
  • Systemic mastocytosis is usually diagnosed after the second decade; male to female ratio 1:1 to 1:1.5.
  • Cause unknown; rare familial cases.

14.3Pathogenesis and clinical picture

  • KIT D816V (aspartate to valine at codon 816) or another gain-of-function KIT mutation in about 90%: more mast cell proliferation, survival and activation.
  • Advanced disease and SM-AHN often add TET2, SRSF2, ASXL1, RUNX1, JAK2.
  • Degranulation (mast cells bind IgE with high affinity) releases histamine, tryptase, proteoglycans, prostaglandins, leukotrienes and cytokines.
  1. Constitutional: fatigue, weight loss, fever, sweats.
  2. Skin: pruritus, urticaria, dermatographism, flushing.
  3. Mediator events: abdominal pain, diarrhoea, syncope, headache, hypotension, tachycardia, wheeze.
  4. Musculoskeletal: bone pain, osteopenia or osteoporosis, fractures, arthralgia, myalgia.
  5. Organ damage in high-grade disease; organomegaly in advanced SM (spleen usually minimal, nodes and liver less often).

14.5Laboratory diagnosis

  1. CBC: anaemia, leucocytosis with eosinophilia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia.
  2. Blood film: circulating mast cells are rare except in mast cell leukaemia.
  3. Marrow aspirate and trephine (Giemsa or toluidine blue): multifocal clusters or diffuse infiltrates of spindle-shaped mast cells with oval nuclei and pale, hypogranular cytoplasm. Promastocytes (bi- or multilobed nuclei) suggest aggressive disease; metachromatic blasts occur in mast cell leukaemia; mitoses are rare even in aggressive forms.
  4. Cytochemistry: tryptase and specific (chloroacetate) esterase positive.
  5. Immunophenotype: CD117 (KIT) positive; neoplastic mast cells co-express CD25, less often CD2, some CD30.
  6. Genetics: KIT D816V (≈90%) or other KIT mutations; KIT outside the mast cell lineage is adverse.
  7. Biochemistry: high serum tryptase, urinary histamine metabolites, prostaglandin D₂, calcitonin; abnormal liver tests.
  8. Tissue biopsy (skin, gut) with toluidine blue or tryptase.

14.6Diagnostic criteria for systemic mastocytosis

The major criterion plus one minor, or three minor criteria.

  • Major: multifocal dense infiltrates of mast cells (≥15 mast cells in aggregates) in marrow or another extracutaneous organ.
  • Minor 1: >25% of mast cells atypical (type I or II) on smears, or spindle-shaped in infiltrates (ignore atypia next to marrow particles).
  • Minor 2: activating KIT mutation at codon 816 or another critical region.
  • Minor 3: aberrant CD2, CD25 or CD30 on mast cells.
  • Minor 4: baseline serum tryptase >20 ng/mL without an associated myeloid neoplasm. In hereditary α-tryptasaemia divide by 1 plus the extra α-tryptase gene copies.

Mast cell leukaemiaDiffuse dense marrow infiltrate, ≥20% mast cells on aspirate; classic form ≥10% in blood, aleukaemic form (<10%) is commoner; CD117, CD25, CD2; CD34, CD123, HLA-DR are adverse; survival usually <1 year
Prognosis and differential diagnosis
  • Prognosis follows the KIT variant allele fraction. ISM and SSM: near-normal life expectancy. ASM, SM-AHN and MCL: poor, better with no cytopenias, low KIT VAF, no extra mutations, atypical KIT mutations or PDGFR rearrangement. SRSF2, ASXL1 and RUNX1 are adverse.
  • Monoclonal mast cell activation syndrome: severe mediator attacks with high tryptase and clonal markers (KIT D816V or CD25), but SM criteria not met.
  • Reactive mast cell hyperplasia: loosely scattered, round, well-granulated mast cells; dense aggregates are rare.

Treatment in Dr Abeer's notes for mast cell leukaemia: anti-IgE, chemotherapy and stem cell transplantation; mediator symptoms are controlled with antihistamines and trigger avoidance.
