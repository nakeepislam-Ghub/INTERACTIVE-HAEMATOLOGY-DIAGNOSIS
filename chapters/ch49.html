Bone marrow structure and examination

Marrow structure and cellularity, when to choose an aspirate or a trephine, the normal differential count, iron stain and reticulin.

F6.1Structure

Haematopoietic tissue fills the spaces between bony trabeculae, supported by a stromal network of reticulin fibres, adipocytes, fibroblastic reticular cells, macrophages and a rich sinusoidal circulation. In a trephine section, the different lineages have typical locations:

  • Granulocyte precursors mature from the paratrabecular and perivascular regions towards the centre of the intertrabecular space.
  • Erythroid islands sit centrally, often near sinusoids, around a macrophage.
  • Megakaryocytes lie next to sinusoids, away from the bone surface. Paratrabecular megakaryocytes or tight clusters are abnormal.
  • Lymphoid aggregates can be found in normal marrow, more often in older people. They are small, well-circumscribed and not paratrabecular.

F6.2Cellularity

Cellularity is the proportion of the marrow space occupied by haematopoietic cells rather than fat. A rough guide is 100 minus the age in years: close to 100% at birth, about 50% to 70% in young adults and 30% to 40% or less in the elderly. Subcortical marrow is often hypocellular even in healthy people, so cellularity should be judged on intertrabecular spaces deeper in the core.

F6.5Iron stain (Perls Prussian blue)

Storage iron is assessed in macrophages within particles and graded (for example 0 to 6 in the Gale scale). Normal sideroblasts, with one to a few fine blue granules, make up about 20% to 50% of erythroblasts. A ring sideroblast has 5 or more granules covering at least one third of the nuclear circumference; ring sideroblasts are not seen in normal marrow.

F6.6Reticulin

Graded on a silver stain from MF-0 to MF-3 (European consensus, used by WHO). Normal marrow is MF-0 to MF-1: scattered linear reticulin without intersections, mainly around vessels.

F6.7Key points

  • Cellularity rough guide: 100 minus age; ignore subcortical spaces
  • Megakaryocytes near sinusoids; paratrabecular location or clusters are abnormal
  • Aspirate: morphology, iron, flow, genetics. Trephine: cellularity, fibrosis, focal lesions
  • Count at least 500 cells; M:E about 2:1 to 4:1; blasts 0–3%; plasma cells 0–3%
  • Ring sideroblast: 5 or more granules covering one third of the nucleus or more
  • Trephine core at least 1.5–2 cm; normal reticulin MF-0 to MF-1
