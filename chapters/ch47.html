Monocytes, macrophages and lymphocytes

How to recognise monocytes, macrophages, lymphocytes, plasma cells and hematogones, and what each one does.

F4.1Monocytes and macrophages

Monocytes are the largest normal leucocytes in blood at 15–20 µm. The nucleus is folded, lobulated or kidney-shaped with a lacy, delicate chromatin pattern. The cytoplasm is grey-blue with a ground-glass look, fine azurophilic granules and often vacuoles. They circulate for 1 to 3 days before entering tissues.

Immunophenotype: CD14, CD64, CD11b, CD33 (bright), CD36, HLA-DR. In tissue, monocyte-derived and resident macrophages include Kupffer cells (liver), alveolar macrophages, splenic red pulp macrophages, microglia and osteoclasts (formed by fusion). In the marrow, macrophages hold storage iron, which is graded on a Perls stain.

F4.2Lymphocytes

  • Small lymphocyte: 7–10 µm, round nucleus with dense clumped chromatin, thin rim of pale blue cytoplasm.
  • Large lymphocyte: 12–16 µm, more abundant cytoplasm.
  • Large granular lymphocyte (LGL): abundant pale cytoplasm with a few coarse azurophilic granules. These are NK cells or cytotoxic CD8+ T cells and make up 10% to 15% of blood lymphocytes.

Young children have a higher lymphocyte count than adults, so a lymphocyte count that would be raised in an adult can be normal at age 2. Reactive lymphocytes (in viral infections) are larger, with abundant cytoplasm that is darker blue at the edge and scalloped around neighbouring red cells.

F4.3Plasma cells

The end stage of B cell differentiation. Oval cell with an eccentric nucleus, coarse "clock-face" chromatin, deeply basophilic cytoplasm and a pale perinuclear Golgi zone (hof). Normal marrow plasma cells are up to about 3% to 3.5% of nucleated cells, are polytypic and express CD38 (bright), CD138, CD19 and CD45, without CD56. Loss of CD19 and gain of CD56 point to a neoplastic population, which Module 3 covers.

F4.4Hematogones

Normal B cell precursors in the marrow. They look like small to medium blasts with scant cytoplasm and smooth chromatin, and are prominent in children, in regenerating marrow after chemotherapy and after transplant. On flow cytometry they show an orderly maturation pattern (CD34 and CD10 in early cells, then CD20 rising), unlike the arrested, uniform pattern of B lymphoblastic leukaemia.

F4.5Key points

  • Monocyte: largest blood leucocyte, folded nucleus, lacy chromatin, grey-blue vacuolated cytoplasm; CD14 and CD64
  • Blood lymphocytes: T 60–80%, B 10–20%, NK 10–15%; CD4:CD8 about 2:1
  • LGLs are NK or cytotoxic T cells with azurophilic granules
  • Plasma cells: eccentric nucleus, clock-face chromatin, hof; normal is CD19+ CD56−, up to about 3%
  • Hematogones show orderly maturation on flow, unlike B-ALL
