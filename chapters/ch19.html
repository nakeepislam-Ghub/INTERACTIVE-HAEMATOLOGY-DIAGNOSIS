Secondary myeloid neoplasms and myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia

Myeloid neoplasms after cytotoxic therapy, with germline predisposition and in Down syndrome; then the tyrosine kinase fusion neoplasms that often present with eosinophilia.

19.1Secondary myeloid neoplasms

Myeloid neoplasms that arise after exposure to cytotoxic therapy or on a background of germline predisposition. WHO 2022 groups three families, and classifies them before any de novo type:

  • Myeloid neoplasms post cytotoxic therapy (pCT)
  • Myeloid neoplasms associated with germline predisposition
  • Myeloid proliferations associated with Down syndrome

19.2Myeloid neoplasms post cytotoxic therapy

MDS, MDS/MPN or AML in a patient previously given DNA-damaging chemotherapy or large-field radiotherapy. Progression of an MPN, and evolution of a primary MDS or MDS/MPN to AML, are excluded. They make up 10–20% of AML, MDS and MDS/MPN, mostly after treatment of solid tumours or lymphoid malignancy, sometimes after autoimmune disease.

Agents

  • Alkylating agents: melphalan, cyclophosphamide, chlorambucil, busulfan, nitrogen mustard, platinum drugs, dacarbazine, procarbazine, carmustine, lomustine, thiotepa, mitomycin C.
  • Ionising radiation to large fields containing active marrow.
  • Topoisomerase II inhibitors: etoposide, teniposide, doxorubicin, daunorubicin, mitoxantrone, amsacrine, actinomycin.
  • Others: antimetabolites (thiopurines, fludarabine, azathioprine, mycophenolate), antitubulin agents in combination, PARP1 inhibitors; growth factors given with chemotherapy. The role of hydroxyurea, radioisotopes, L-asparaginase and limited-field radiotherapy is unclear.

Pathogenesis: therapy damages DNA-repair and damage-sensing genes and selects pre-existing clones, especially clonal haemopoiesis with TP53, PPM1D, DNMT3A, ASXL1 or TET2; drug-metabolism polymorphisms (NQO1) and inherited DNA-repair or Fanconi pathway variants add risk.

19.3Myeloid neoplasms with germline predisposition

AML, MDS, MPN and MDS/MPN arising in people with inherited (pathogenic or likely pathogenic) variants. Extra somatic hits are needed for transformation.

  • Children and adolescents: germline SAMD9, SAMD9L and GATA2 are the commonest causes of MDS, especially with monosomy 7.
  • Adults 18–40: DNA-repair and telomere biology disorders cause MDS (19%), aplastic anaemia (15%) and AML (≥5%).
  • Older adults: germline DDX41 typically causes MDS.
  • Marrow: aplastic, MDS/AML-like, or normal; usually hypocellular; multilineage dysplasia; peritrabecular myeloid expansion with promyelocyte arrest and eosinophilia (in severe congenital neutropenia); reticulin fibrosis.
  • Tests: telomere length (telomere disorders), chromosome breakage (Fanconi anaemia), and germline sequencing on cultured skin fibroblasts or hair bulbs (blood or marrow contain the somatic clone).
  • Criteria: a germline variant plus a myeloid neoplasm (MDS features or ≥20% blasts); additional clonal changes and a family history are desirable.
  • Management: most need transplant (screen related donors for the same variant); organ dysfunction limits chemotherapy. Otherwise the outlook mirrors sporadic disease.

WHO groups them as: without a pre-existing disorder (CEBPA, DDX41, TP53/Li–Fraumeni), with a pre-existing platelet disorder (RUNX1, ANKRD26, ETV6), and with potential organ dysfunction (GATA2, SAMD9/9L, bone-marrow failure and RASopathies). Explore them all in the panel below.

19.4Myeloid proliferations associated with Down syndrome

Children with constitutional trisomy 21 have a 150-fold higher risk of AML. A somatic GATA1 exon 2/3 mutation produces a short protein (GATA1s) that is leukaemogenic only in trisomy 21.

19.5Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia and tyrosine kinase fusions

Stem cell neoplasms driven by a fused, constitutively active tyrosine kinase. Eosinophilia is usual but not invariable, and the disease can present as an MPN, MDS/MPN, AML, T- or B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma, or a mixed phenotype, and switch between them. ≥20% blasts at any time is called blast phase of the same entity, and systemic mastocytosis with one of these fusions is classified here.

PDGFRA

  • Strong male predominance (7 : 1), median age 40s. FIP1L1::PDGFRA from a cryptic 4q12 deletion (not seen on karyotype: use FISH or PCR).
  • Fatigue, itch, respiratory, cardiac and gut symptoms, hepatosplenomegaly; endomyocardial fibrosis, valve damage, thrombosis.
  • Eosinophilia, neutrophilia; marrow with eosinophils, Charcot–Leyden crystals and loose CD25+ mast cells (CD2−); fibrosis. Serum tryptase ≥12 ng/mL and markedly raised B12.
  • Criteria: myeloid or lymphoid neoplasm with eosinophilia plus a PDGFRA fusion. If testing is unavailable, suspect it in a BCR::ABL1-negative MPN with eosinophilia, splenomegaly, high B12 and tryptase.
  • Imatinib works in chronic and blast phase. Worse with blast phase at onset, complex karyotype, cardiac involvement.

PDGFRB

  • Men, 40s. ETV6::PDGFRB, t(5;12)(q32;p13.2), commonest. Usually looks like CMML with eosinophilia, MDS/MPN-N or chronic eosinophilic leukaemia; leucocytosis, monocytosis, no basophilia, no thrombocytopenia; spindled mast cells and fibrosis.
  • Extremely sensitive to imatinib. A BCR::ABL1-like B-ALL with PDGFRB but no myeloid features stays B-ALL.

FGFR1 (8p11)

  • Median age 30s. Partner shapes the disease: ZMYM2 (t(8;13)) gives T-lymphoblastic lymphoma with an MPN; BCR and TPR give MPN with basophilia; CEP43 and CNTRL give CMML-like disease.
  • Lymphadenopathy, mediastinal mass, splenomegaly, B symptoms; often bi- or multilineal. Aggressive and resistant to imatinib: transplant early.

JAK2

  • Men (27 : 5), 50s. PCM1::JAK2, t(8;9), commonest; also ETV6 and BCR partners. Triad: hypercellular marrow with eosinophilia, large clusters of immature erythroid precursors, and myelofibrosis.
  • Resistant to imatinib; ruxolitinib responses are short (relapse in 1–2 years): transplant early.

FLT3, ETV6::ABL1 and other kinases

  • FLT3 (13q12; ETV6 commonest partner): MDS, MPN, CMML, or T/B lymphoblastic disease; lymphadenopathy, lytic lesions. Aggressive.
  • ETV6::ABL1, t(9;12), often cryptic: CML-like with eosinophilia and basophilia; responds to ABL1 inhibitors.
  • Other fusions (ETV6::FGFR2, ETV6::LYN, ETV6::NTRK3, RANBP2::ALK, BCR::RET): eosinophilia, monocytosis, spindled CD25+ mast cells; T-lymphoid component more common. Found by FISH and RNA sequencing.
