Plasma cell neoplasms and other paraprotein diseases

Monoclonal gammopathies (MGUS, cold agglutinin disease, MGRS), amyloid and deposition diseases, heavy chain diseases, plasmacytoma, myeloma and its variants, and the POEMS, TEMPI and AESOP syndromes.

24.1Paraproteins: the families

Mature B-cell or plasma cell clones that secrete an M protein: an intact monoclonal Ig, a free light chain, rarely a truncated heavy chain, or a misfolded Ig. WHO 2022 groups them as:

  • Monoclonal gammopathies: cold agglutinin disease, IgM MGUS, non-IgM MGUS, monoclonal gammopathy of renal significance (MGRS).
  • Monoclonal Ig deposition diseases: AL amyloidosis, monoclonal immunoglobulin deposition disease (MIDD).
  • Heavy chain diseases: μ, γ, α.
  • Plasma cell neoplasms: plasmacytoma, plasma cell myeloma (smouldering, non-secretory, plasma cell leukaemia), and those with paraneoplastic syndromes (POEMS, TEMPI now confirmed, AESOP new).

24.2Monoclonal gammopathies

Cold agglutinin disease (CAD)

An autoimmune haemolytic anaemia caused by a small, indolent B-cell clone making IgM κ anti-I that binds red cells best at 0–4 °C, without infection or overt lymphoma. Commoner in cold countries; late 60s.

  • IgM agglutinates red cells in cool peripheries (acrocyanosis, Raynaud, livedo) and fixes complement: extravascular (C3b, liver) and some intravascular haemolysis.
  • Film: red cell agglutination, polychromasia. Marrow: nodular aggregates of small B cells, plasma cells <5%. Trisomy 3 or 12/18 gains (70%), KMT2D and CARD11 mutations; no MYD88 (unlike LPL).
  • Essential: chronic haemolysis; DAT strongly positive for C3d (IgG negative or weak); cold agglutinin titre ≥64 at 4 °C; no overt cancer or infection (Mycoplasma, EBV); a clonal B-cell population. Keep samples at 37 °C until serum is separated.

  • Light-chain MGUS: abnormal FLC ratio (<0.26 or >1.65) with a raised involved light chain and no heavy chain on immunofixation.
  • Both are commoner with age, in men and in Black people. Progression is about 1% a year; high risk with an abnormal FLC ratio, IgA or IgM type, and M protein >1.5 g/dL.

Monoclonal gammopathy of renal significance (MGRS)

A clone that is not malignant by size but whose Ig damages the kidney: AL amyloid, MIDD, light-chain proximal tubulopathy, crystal-storing histiocytosis, cryoglobulinaemia (usually B-cell clones), C3 glomerulopathy, proliferative GN with monoclonal deposits, thrombotic microangiopathy. Cast nephropathy is not MGRS: it is a myeloma-defining event.

Essential: kidney biopsy showing injury from a monoclonal Ig; proteinuria >1 g/day (mostly albumin); progressive kidney injury. Desirable: no myeloma features (no lytic lesions, plasmacytoma, hypercalcaemia, Hb <10, marrow PCs <60%, FLC ratio <100).

24.3Amyloid, deposition and heavy chain diseases

AL amyloidosis

  • Unstable light chains (usually λ, >80%) misfold into 9 nm non-branching fibrils that bind glycosaminoglycans and serum amyloid P. One-sixth as common as myeloma.
  • Clinical: heart failure with thick walls and preserved ejection fraction, nephrotic syndrome, neuropathy, autonomic failure, bilateral carpal tunnel, hepatomegaly, macroglossia, periorbital purpura.
  • Tissue: pink waxy material with cracking; Congo red apple-green birefringence under polarised light; fat pad, marrow, salivary gland or organ biopsy (not FNA). Type by laser microdissection and mass spectrometry; exclude hereditary forms (transthyretin, ApoA1, fibrinogen, lysozyme, gelsolin).
  • Marrow: modest clonal plasma cells (median 9%), CD56, cyclin D1 (t(11;14) common).
  • Staging: NT-proBNP, troponin and FLC difference (Mayo 2004 European modification; Mayo 2012). Advanced cardiac disease has high early mortality.

Monoclonal immunoglobulin deposition disease (MIDD)

Non-amyloid, Congo red-negative, PAS-positive linear deposits of light chain (80%), light + heavy, or heavy chain along tubular and glomerular basement membranes, vessels, heart, liver and lung. Nodular glomerulosclerosis in two-thirds; powdery electron-dense deposits on EM. Over 95% are from plasma cell neoplasms; t(11;14) in about half.

Heavy chain diseases

Truncated heavy chains (lost VH/CH1 domain) that cannot bind light chain; may give no clear spike, so immunofixation is needed, and IGH PCR can be falsely negative.

24.4Plasmacytoma

  • A single lesion of clonal plasma cells without myeloma: solitary plasmacytoma of bone (spine, pelvis, ribs, skull) or extramedullary (20–30%; upper airways: nose, sinuses, nasopharynx, larynx). 2–5% of plasma cell neoplasms.
  • Pain, swelling, fracture or cord compression. M protein in half (IgG > IgA).
  • Sheets of plasma cells (mature to plasmablastic, Dutcher bodies); CD56 and cyclin D1 rare. EBV-positive cases in immunocompetent people (EPIC) are rare.
  • Essential: biopsy-proven clonal plasma cell lesion; no clonal B cells; no other lesions on imaging; no CRAB; <10% clonal marrow plasma cells (1–9% = "with minimal marrow involvement", higher risk).
  • Differential: lymphoma with plasmacytic differentiation (B cells, IgM), plasmablastic lymphoma (oral, EBV, MYC), neuroendocrine tumour (keratins, NE markers).
  • Radiotherapy. Progression to myeloma is higher in bone than extramedullary disease, and with lesions >5 cm, older age, marrow involvement and paraprotein persisting >1 year.

24.5Plasma cell myeloma: biology

A marrow neoplasm of class-switched, Ig-secreting plasma cells, usually with an M protein and organ damage. 1.8% of new cancers; twice as common in Black people; first-degree relatives 2–4×. Almost all arise from MGUS.

  • Primary genetics: hyperdiploid (45%; trisomies of odd chromosomes 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 15, 19, 21) or IGH translocations (40%): t(11;14) CCND1, t(4;14) NSD2/FGFR3, t(14;16) MAF, t(6;14) CCND3, t(14;20) MAFB.
  • Secondary: del(13q)/monosomy 13, del(17p) (TP53), del(1p), 1q gain, MYC rearrangement; RAS/MAPK and NF-κB mutations.
  • Microenvironment: SDF-1 to CXCR4 homing; CD138, CD38, CD40 adhesion; VLA-4 (β1 integrin) to VCAM-1 gives drug resistance; IL-6, TNF-α, IGF-1, VEGF support growth and bone resorption.

24.6Plasma cell myeloma: diagnosis

  • Blood: normocytic anaemia; film with rouleaux and a blue background; sometimes leucoerythroblastic; ESR very high.
  • Biochemistry: high total protein, low albumin (15%), hypercalcaemia (10%), raised creatinine (20–30%), urate, β2-microglobulin, LDH.
  • Electrophoresis and immunofixation: M band in 97%: IgG 50%, IgA 20%, light chain only 20%, IgD/IgE/IgM/biclonal <10%; immunoparesis in 90%. Serum free light chains; 24-hour urine for Bence Jones protein; mass spectrometry is most sensitive.
  • Imaging: whole-body low-dose CT, PET-CT or MRI. Lytic lesions in vertebrae, ribs, skull, pelvis, proximal long bones.
  • Marrow aspirate: plasma cells from slightly raised to 90%: mature (clock-face chromatin, perinuclear hof), lymphoplasmacytoid, plasmablastic (10%, adverse), multinucleated; inclusions: Mott/morula cells (Russell bodies), flame cells (IgA), pseudo-Gaucher cells, crystals. Nuclear immaturity is rare in reactive plasmacytosis; inclusions are not specific.
  • Trephine: interstitial, clusters, nodules or sheets; ≥30% plasma cells or sheets favour myeloma. Patchy disease can give a low aspirate count: target the lesion.
  • Flow: CD38 bright, CD138, CD229, CD319 (SLAMF7), cytoplasmic light-chain restriction, CD79a; CD19−, CD45 dim/−, CD27 and CD81 low; aberrant CD56 (60–80%), CD117, CD28, CD200, CD20, CD33. CD19 + CD81 is favourable. MRD by next-generation flow or sequencing.

Prognosis: survival from months to over 10 years. Adverse: ISS III, high-risk genetics [t(4;14), t(14;16), del(17p), 1q gain, del(1p); bi-allelic TP53], CD19/CD28/CD200 expression or CD45/CD117 loss, plasmablastic morphology, heavy marrow replacement, high LDH and CRP, age >70, frailty, and poor response.

Smouldering myeloma: who progresses?

Risk is highest in the first years and falls with time. Adverse: marrow plasma cells over 20% (especially with over 95% aberrant phenotype), rising circulating plasma cells, M protein over 2–3 g/dL, IgA type, immunoparesis, involved:uninvolved FLC ratio over 20, and t(4;14), t(14;16), 1q gain, del(17p) or del(13q). Try the 20/2/20 calculator. Light-chain smouldering myeloma has 10–60% clonal plasma cells and urinary light chain of at least 500 mg/24 h.

Follow-up: CBC, creatinine, calcium, serum and urine electrophoresis and FLC after 2–3 months; if stable every 4–6 months for a year, then every 6–12 months.

Measurable residual disease in myeloma
  • When: after induction, transplant and consolidation; at the end of therapy on an adequate first-pull aspirate; yearly on maintenance until negative twice.
  • Next-generation flow (10⁻⁵): find plasma cells by CD38, CD138 and CD45/side scatter, then separate clonal from normal by CD19, CD56, CD117, CD20, CD27, CD28, CD81, CD200 and cytoplasmic light chain restriction. Pitfalls: anti-CD38 antibodies (daratumumab) and loss of plasma cells during processing.
  • NGS of the IG rearrangement reaches 10⁻⁶. PET/CT adds imaging MRD outside the marrow.

Differential diagnosis

  • Reactive plasmacytosis (infection, autoimmune disease, liver disease): polyclonal, rarely ≥30%, no nuclear immaturity.
  • B-cell lymphoma with plasmacytic differentiation (LPL, MZL): CD19+, sIg+ B cells; IgM; MYD88.
  • Hyperviscosity: Waldenström (IgM; CD19+, CD38−, CD138− lymphoid cells), polycythaemia, thrombocytosis, extreme leucocytosis.
