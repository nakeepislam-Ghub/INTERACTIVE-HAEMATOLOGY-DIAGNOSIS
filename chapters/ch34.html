34.1Vitamin K antagonists (warfarin)

Inhibit vitamin K epoxide reductase, so factors II, VII, IX and X and proteins C, S and Z are released uncarboxylated (PIVKAs) and cannot bind phospholipid.

  • Each factor falls at its own half-life: VII first, II last. The early PT rise is not full anticoagulation.
  • Protein C also falls early, a brief hypercoagulable phase: give LMWH for at least 5 days, until the INR has been in range for 48 hours.
  • Monitor with the INR; target 2–3 (about 2.5), higher for mechanical valves.
What changes the warfarin effect
  • Increases: CYP2C9 and VKORC1 variants, liver disease, diarrhoea, malnutrition; enzyme inhibitors (clarithromycin, cimetidine), protein-binding displacement, antibiotics (less gut vitamin K), alcohol, thyroid hormone, clofibrate.
  • Decreases: enzyme inducers (rifampicin, barbiturates), cholestyramine, vitamin K and lipid emulsions, hereditary resistance.
  • Aspirin and NSAIDs add bleeding risk without changing the INR.

Use the overdose guide for a high INR.

Monitoring heparin

LMWH has a predictable response and needs monitoring only in renal failure, pregnancy, obesity and children (peak anti-Xa 0.5–1.0 IU/mL, 4–6 hours after the dose). Unfractionated heparin binds plasma proteins unpredictably and must be monitored.

Heparin resistance

  • True: high heparin-binding proteins, fast clearance, or low antithrombin (congenital, or acquired in compensated DIC). Use a direct thrombin inhibitor, or give antithrombin.
  • Apparent: the APTT stays short because acute-phase factor VIII is high, but the anti-Xa is in range. Monitor by anti-Xa instead.

Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia

  • Type I: mild, non-immune fall in the first 1–2 days; harmless.
  • Type II: IgG against PF4–heparin activates platelets; count falls >50% on days 5–14 (earlier with heparin in the last 100 days); thrombosis in up to half.
  • Score with the 4Ts (Chapter 30). ELISA for anti-PF4/heparin; the serotonin release assay is the gold-standard functional test.
  • Stop all heparin; use argatroban, bivalirudin, danaparoid or fondaparinux. Do not start warfarin until the platelets recover.

34.3Direct thrombin and Xa inhibitors

  • Hirudin (irreversible), bivalirudin and argatroban (reversible, hepatic clearance): IV; monitor with APTT 1.5–3 × control; reptilase time unaffected.
  • Dabigatran: oral thrombin inhibitor, renal clearance; reversed by idarucizumab. Prolongs TT strongly.
  • Rivaroxaban (renal) and apixaban (biliary): oral Xa inhibitors; measured by drug-calibrated anti-Xa; reversed by andexanet alfa or PCC.
When to measure a direct oral anticoagulant

Fixed doses need no routine monitoring. Measure when there is bleeding, new thrombosis on treatment, suspected overdose or poor compliance, interacting drugs, bridging, urgent or elective surgery, neuraxial anaesthesia, thrombolysis being considered, gut surgery (absorption), renal impairment, extremes of body weight or very old age.

Effects on PT and APTT vary with the reagent and between drugs of the same class. Use drug-calibrated anti-Xa for Xa inhibitors and dilute thrombin time or ecarin clotting time for dabigatran; LC–MS is the reference method but is not a rapid test.

34.4Thrombolytics, antifibrinolytics and antiplatelets

Thrombolytic therapy

Given in the first hours for ischaemic stroke, myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, extensive DVT and local arterial thrombi. Avoid if platelets are below 100 × 10⁹/L, INR above 1.5 on warfarin, APTT prolonged on heparin, or glucose below 40 or above 400 mg/dL.

Monitoring (only beyond 24 hours; citrate tube with a fibrinolysis inhibitor): APTT about 1.5 × control, TT 40 s or more, fibrinogen variable, FDPs high. Bleeding comes with low fibrinogen, V and VIII: stop the drug, give tranexamic acid or EACA, FFP and cryoprecipitate.

Other drugs

  • Antifibrinolytics: tranexamic acid and EACA block plasminogen's lysine-binding site; aprotinin inhibits serine proteases. Contraindicated in DIC.
  • Aspirin: irreversibly acetylates COX-1 for the platelet's life; absent arachidonic acid aggregation for 7–10 days.
  • NSAIDs inhibit COX reversibly for hours, but they block aspirin's access to COX-1: give aspirin 2 hours before them.
  • Clopidogrel, ticlopidine: block P2Y12; reduced ADP aggregation.
  • GPIIb/IIIa antagonists: abciximab, eptifibatide, tirofiban (can cause acute thrombocytopenia).
  • Dipyridamole raises cAMP; dextran and starch coat platelets.
