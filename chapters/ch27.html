27.1Flow cytometry in acute leukaemia

Acute leukaemia is a clonal expansion of blasts, arrested in differentiation, in marrow, blood or other tissues. Flow cytometry:

  • Confirms that blasts are present and helps count them in blood, but not in marrow (haemodilution and variable red cell lysis): count marrow blasts on the smear.
  • Determines lineage. The most useful markers: AML CD13, CD33, CD117 (and MPO); B-ALL CD10, CD19, cytoplasmic CD79a; T-ALL cytoplasmic CD3.
  • Recognises acute undifferentiated leukaemia: CD34, TdT, HLA-DR or CD38 with no cCD3, MPO, cCD22, cCD79a or strong CD19.
  • Defines the leukaemia-associated phenotype used later for minimal residual disease.

27.3B- and T-lymphoblastic leukaemia

B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma

  • Low SSC, CD45 dim (may be negative or moderate).
  • TdT+, CD34+, HLA-DR+, CD117−.
  • B lineage: strong CD19 plus one of cCD79a, cCD22, CD10, or weak CD19 plus two of them. CD19 and cytoplasmic CD22 are the most sensitive B markers.
  • Typical: CD19+, CD22+, CD79a+, CD10 bright, CD20−; no surface light chain.
  • Aberrant CD13, CD33, CD56 or CD117 occasionally.

27.4Acute leukaemia of ambiguous lineage

  • Acute undifferentiated leukaemia (AUL): no lineage-specific antigen.
  • Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (MPAL): antigens of more than one lineage, as distinct populations each of one lineage (bilineal), one population carrying several lineages (biphenotypic), or a combination.

The older EGIL score gives points per marker (2, 1 or 0.5); a biphenotypic leukaemia scores more than 2 for the myeloid lineage and one lymphoid lineage. The WHO criteria instead require: myeloid = MPO, or monocytic differentiation (at least two of NSE, CD11c, CD14, CD64, lysozyme); T = cytoplasmic (or surface) CD3; B = strong CD19 with one of CD79a, cCD22, CD10, or weak CD19 with two. 27.5Mature B-cell and plasma cell neoplasms

Mature lymphoid neoplasms are clonal proliferations of mature B, T or NK cells. Work-up: blood count, film or tissue biopsy, immunophenotyping, genetics and clonality, renal and liver function, LDH, β2-microglobulin, albumin, calcium, uric acid, and lumbar puncture if CNS disease is suspected. The microscope comes first: small mature cells (SLL, MCL, MZL, low-grade FL, LPL) or medium blastoid cells (lymphoblastic lymphoma, blastoid MCL, high-grade B-cell lymphoma, DLBCL).

General features of mature B-cell neoplasms

  • Clonality: one light chain only (κ or λ), best seen on a two-parameter dot plot, ideally within the abnormal population (by size, CD5 or CD10, or CD20/CD19 intensity). The κ+ cells should equal the λ− cells and vice versa.
  • T-cell markers negative; B markers dim to moderate; CD45 bright; HLA-DR+.
  • Surface Ig-negative B-cell neoplasms: plasma cells, CLL/SLL (very dim), follicular lymphoma, PMBL and DLBCL. Do not confuse them with B lymphoblasts and haematogones (CD10+, CD34/TdT+). Try the clonality ratio tool.

27.6Mature T- and NK-cell neoplasms

These are 10–12% of lymphoid malignancies. Flow often can only suspect a T-cell lymphoma, because it cannot prove T-cell clonality; TCR gene studies, morphology, cytogenetics and viral studies complete the picture.

  • Benign T cells: CD2, CD3, CD5, CD7, CD4 or CD8, CD43, CD45, TCRαβ; HLA-DR−. Unusual T-cell populations appear in many reactive states, and one odd antigen on a few cells means little.
  • Suspicious features: high forward scatter (ALCL, large-cell PTCL); abnormal expression of 3 or 4 pan-T markers (or 1–2 with other abnormalities); complete loss is more specific than dim; "smeary" heterogeneous expression; CD7 loss is common.
  • CD45 loss does not occur in reactive T cells (seen in T-PLL, ALCL). TCR: dim αβ, γδ positive, or both absent suggests malignancy.
  • CD4/CD8: normal blood about 2:1. Restriction, double positive or double negative raise suspicion when the T-cell population is expanded; double positive or negative is commoner in T-ALL, CD4+ in mature T neoplasms.
