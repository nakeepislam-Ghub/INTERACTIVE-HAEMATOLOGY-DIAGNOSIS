Disorders of DNA synthesis

Vitamin B12 and folate metabolism, megaloblastic anaemia and pernicious anaemia, with how each is diagnosed and treated.

3.1Vitamin B12 (cobalamin)

  • Structure: a corrin ring with central cobalt. Methylcobalamin (cytoplasm) serves methionine synthase; adenosylcobalamin (mitochondria) serves methylmalonyl-CoA mutase.
  • Sources: animal food only. Needs 1–2 µg a day; liver stores of 2–3 mg last 3–4 years.
  • Active absorption: released from food by acid and pepsin, bound first by haptocorrin (R binder), freed by pancreatic proteases in the duodenum, then bound to intrinsic factor. The IF–B12 complex enters terminal ileal cells through the cubilin–amnionless receptor and reaches the portal blood on transcobalamin II after about 6 hours. B12 also has an enterohepatic circulation.
  • Passive absorption takes about 1% of a large oral dose without IF, which is why high-dose oral B12 works.

The two B12 reactions

  1. Homocysteine → methionine (methionine synthase), which regenerates tetrahydrofolate from 5-methyl-THF and makes S-adenosylmethionine for methylation of proteins, lipids, myelin and neurotransmitters.
  2. Methylmalonyl-CoA → succinyl-CoA (mutase). This is why methylmalonic acid rises only in B12 deficiency.
Inherited and acquired defects of cobalamin metabolism
  • Congenital TCII deficiency: megaloblastic anaemia with a normal serum B12.
  • Cbl defects (cblA–G): methylmalonic aciduria, homocystinuria or both, with failure to thrive and neurological disease from birth.
  • Nitrous oxide oxidises cobalamin and inactivates methionine synthase: acute megaloblastic change with falling white cells and platelets.

3.2Folate

  • Sources: green vegetables, nuts, liver, yeast; destroyed by boiling. Needs about 100 µg a day; stores of 5–10 mg last only about 4 months.
  • Absorption: polyglutamates are cut to monoglutamates at the brush border and absorbed in the duodenum and upper jejunum (PCFT1), then leave as 5-methyl-THF.
  • Trapping: inside cells, B12-dependent methionine synthase turns 5-methyl-THF into THF, which is polyglutamated and kept. Without B12, folate is "trapped" as 5-methyl-THF and leaks out.
  • Functions: one-carbon transfer for purine synthesis, dUMP → dTMP (thymidylate synthase), homocysteine → methionine, serine → glycine and FIGLU → glutamate.
  • MTHFR C677T gives a thermolabile enzyme, high homocysteine, neural tube defects and thrombosis.

3.4Pathogenesis and clinical picture

  • Less dTMP means slow DNA synthesis while RNA and haemoglobin continue: nuclear–cytoplasmic asynchrony. Many precursors die in the marrow (ineffective erythropoiesis).
  • Less S-adenosylmethionine means poor methylation of myelin and DNA.
  1. Marrow: pancytopenia with fatigue, infections and bleeding.
  2. Epithelia: glossitis, macrocytic changes in gut, airway and cervical cells.
  3. Pregnancy: infertility, prematurity, neural tube defects, recurrent loss.
  4. Vessels: high homocysteine causes arterial and venous thrombosis in young people.
  5. Nervous system (B12 only): peripheral neuropathy, subacute combined degeneration of the dorsal and pyramidal tracts, optic atrophy, dementia.
  6. Psychiatric symptoms in both; DNA hypomethylation may predispose to cancer.

3.5Pernicious anaemia

Megaloblastic anaemia from severe lack of intrinsic factor with autoimmune atrophic gastritis.

  • Intrinsic factor antibodies (80%; highly specific): type I blocking (55%) stops B12 binding IF; type II binding (35%) stops the complex attaching to ileum and crosses the placenta.
  • Parietal cell antibodies (90%; less specific: also in relatives, atrophic gastritis, thyroid and Addison disease).
  • Associated with other autoimmune disease; gastric biopsy shows atrophy of body and fundus with an intact antrum.
  • Serum gastrin over 200 ng/L and pepsinogen I under 30 µg/L support the diagnosis.

3.6Laboratory diagnosis

  • Blood count: pancytopenia, MCV over 100 fL (less if iron deficiency or thalassaemia coexist), normal MCHC.
  • Film: oval macrocytes, poikilocytes and fragments, basophilic stippling, Howell–Jolly bodies, Cabot rings; hypersegmented neutrophils are an early sign and disappear about 2 weeks after treatment.
  • Reticulocytes: low for the degree of anaemia.
  • Marrow: hypercellular with megaloblasts (fine, open chromatin with mature haemoglobinised cytoplasm), giant metamyelocytes (persist after treatment), hyperlobated megakaryocytes, increased iron.
  • Ineffective erythropoiesis: very high LDH (LDH1 over LDH2), high indirect bilirubin, low haptoglobin.

The therapeutic trial is diagnostic too: reticulocytes rise from day 3 and peak around day 7; neutrophils and platelets recover in about a week; nerves take up to 6 months. Following MMA and homocysteine shows response better than the B12 level. See the Schilling test simulator for the classic absorption study.

3.7Treatment

  • B12 deficiency: treat the cause; intramuscular hydroxocobalamin.
  • Folate deficiency: oral or parenteral folic acid. Exclude or correct B12 deficiency first, otherwise folate alone can precipitate or worsen B12 neuropathy.
  • Severe anaemia: admission; transfuse cautiously.
