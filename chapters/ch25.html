T-cell and NK-cell neoplasms

T-lymphoblastic and early T-precursor leukaemia, the mature T and NK leukaemias (T-PLL, LGL leukaemias, ATLL, Sézary syndrome, aggressive NK-cell leukaemia), and the cutaneous, intestinal, nodal and EBV-driven T/NK lymphomas.

25.1The WHO 2022 map of T and NK disease

  • Tumour-like lesions: Kikuchi–Fujimoto disease (self-limiting; paracortical immunoblasts, crescentic histiocytes, apoptotic debris, no neutrophils), indolent T-lymphoblastic proliferation (polyclonal, LMO2-negative extrathymic T lymphoblasts), and ALPS (FAS pathway defect; CD4−CD8− αβ T cells >1.5% of lymphocytes, autoimmune cytopenias, raised vitamin B12, sFASL, IL-10).
  • Precursor: T-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma, NOS and early T-precursor ALL/LBL.
  • Mature leukaemic: T-PLL, T-LGLL, NK-LGLL, ATLL, Sézary syndrome, aggressive NK-cell leukaemia.
  • Cutaneous, intestinal, hepatosplenic, ALCL, nodal TFH, PTCL NOS, EBV+ NK/T lymphomas and EBV+ lymphoproliferations of childhood.

25.2T-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma

  • 15% of childhood and 25% of adult ALL; adolescent boys. Many treatment protocols call it leukaemia when marrow blasts exceed 25%.
  • Typically a rapidly growing mediastinal (thymic) mass with effusions, high WBC, nodes and organomegaly; marrow haemopoiesis is relatively spared compared with B-ALL.
  • Blasts: small to medium, convoluted nuclei; MPO negative, PAS coarse blocks, focal acid phosphatase/Golgi NSE.
  • Phenotype: cytoplasmic CD3 (lineage-defining), CD7 bright, CD2, CD5 (dimmer than normal), CD45 lower than T cells; immaturity markers TdT, CD1a, CD34 (one-third), CD99, CD117; LMO2 and TAL1 nuclear staining; HLA-DR negative. Stages pro-T, pre-T, cortical (CD1a+, CD4+CD8+), medullary (sCD3+, single CD4 or CD8).
  • Genetics: NOTCH1/FBXW7 activation; TCR-locus translocations with TLX1 (favourable), TLX3, TAL1 (often STIL::TAL1 deletion), LMO1/2, NKX2-1, HOXA (KMT2A, MLLT10, PICALM::MLLT10); CDKN2A deletion; JAK-STAT (IL7R, JAK1/3), PI3K (PTEN), ABL1 (NUP214::ABL1, a TKI target).
  • Outcome is now better than B-ALL in children (about 85%; 60% in adults); end-of-consolidation MRD is the strongest predictor.
  • Prognosis detail: adverse with older age, complex karyotype, no NOTCH1/FBXW7 mutation or a RAS/PTEN change, and disease after prior chemotherapy; favourable with a cortical (CD1a+) phenotype and NOTCH1/FBXW7 mutation.
  • MRD methods: flow cytometry (0.01–0.1%) needs a wide panel (CD1a, CD2, surface and cytoplasmic CD3, CD4, CD5, CD7, CD8, CD10, CD13, CD33, CD45, CD56, CD99, CD117) because TdT, CD99 and other immaturity markers may disappear during induction; PCR or NGS of the clonal TRB, TRG or TRD rearrangement.

Early T-precursor ALL (ETP-ALL)

12–17% of paediatric and 22–40% of adult T-ALL. Blasts from the earliest thymic immigrant keep stem and myeloid programmes: cCD3+, CD7+; CD1a and CD8 <5%; CD5 <75% (or dim); ≥25% of blasts positive for ≥1 of CD34, HLA-DR, CD117, CD13, CD33, CD11b, CD65; MPO <3% (cytochemistry) or <10% (flow). CD5 on ≥75% = near-ETP. Mutations resemble AML (FLT3, NRAS, KRAS, IL7R, JAK3, RUNX1, GATA3, ETV6, EZH2); NOTCH1 and CDKN2A loss uncommon; germline RUNX1 in some. More MRD positivity, but intensified therapy and transplant give good long-term results. Try the ETP checker.

  • T-PLL essential: lymphocytes >5 × 10⁹/L with T-PLL phenotype, T-cell clonality, and TCL1A/MTCP1 rearrangement or TCL1A protein.
  • T-LGLL: 2 of 3 essential criteria (raised cytotoxic T cells, usually >2 × 10⁹/L; CD8 T cells with CD5/CD7 loss or NK-receptor expression; clonality) plus 1 desirable (intrasinusoidal CD8 marrow infiltrate or STAT3 mutation). Separate from reactive CD8 expansions (viral infection, autoimmunity, post-transplant).
  • NK-LGLL: NK cells >2 × 10⁹/L for >6 months, uniform sCD3−CD16+ population, KIR restriction as a surrogate of clonality, intrasinusoidal NK infiltrate or STAT3/TET2 mutation. EBV negative (unlike ANKL).
  • Sézary blood involvement: clonal TCR with Sézary cells ≥1000/µL, or CD4/CD8 >10, CD4+CD7− ≥40%, or CD4+CD26− ≥30%.

Adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma (Shimoyama)

Only 3–5% of HTLV-1 carriers develop ATLL, decades after infection (often breast-milk transmission). Viral Tax drives NF-κB and AP-1; HBZ drives proliferation and CCR4 expression. LDH, soluble IL-2 receptor and calcium are high.

Acute is diagnosed by exclusion. Chronic is unfavourable if LDH or BUN is high or albumin low. Acute, lymphoma and unfavourable chronic are aggressive; favourable chronic and smouldering are indolent. Try the calculator.

Aggressive NK-cell leukaemia essential: acute febrile illness, systemic proliferation of NK cells, no TCR protein or clonal TR rearrangement; EBER positive in ~90%, HLH. Intravascular NK/T-cell lymphoma is provisionally included.

25.5Intestinal, hepatosplenic and anaplastic lymphomas

  • Enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL): coeliac disease; pleomorphic medium-large cells, eosinophil-rich background; CD4−CD8−, cytotoxic, CD30 often; uninvolved mucosa shows villous atrophy; JAK1/STAT3. Aggressive.
  • Monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T-cell lymphoma (MEITL): no coeliac link; monomorphic CD8+ CD56+ epitheliotropic cells; JAK3, STAT5B, SETD2.
  • Indolent T-cell lymphoma and indolent NK-cell LPD of the GI tract: superficial mucosal infiltrates, low Ki-67; not to be over-treated.
  • Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma: young men, Crohn's disease on thiopurines/anti-TNF; sinusoidal infiltration of marrow, liver and spleen without nodes; CD4−, CD5−, CD8 ±, CD56 ±, usually γδ; isochromosome 7q, trisomy 8. Very poor.

25.6Nodal TFH, PTCL NOS and EBV-driven NK/T lymphomas

  • Nodal TFH cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type (old AITL): elderly; generalised nodes, rash, polyclonal hypergammaglobulinaemia, Coombs-positive haemolysis, eosinophilia. Node: polymorphous infiltrate, arborising high endothelial venules, expanded extrafollicular FDC meshworks (CD21/CD23), EBV+ B immunoblasts. Tumour cells CD4+ with ≥2 TFH markers (PD1, ICOS, CXCL13, BCL6, CD10). TET2, DNMT3A, RHOA G17V, IDH2 R172. Follicular-type and NOS variants share the TFH phenotype.
  • PTCL, NOS: diagnosis of exclusion; aberrant T cells with ≤1 TFH marker and EBV only in scattered B cells; PTCL-GATA3 (worse) and PTCL-TBX21 subgroups.
  • Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma: East Asia, Latin America; nasal destruction, midline facial necrosis; angiocentric, angiodestructive with necrosis; CD56+, cCD3ε+, cytotoxic; EBER in most cells. Asparaginase-based therapy and radiotherapy; plasma EBV DNA tracks disease.
  • EBV+ nodal T/NK-cell lymphoma: nodal presentation of an EBV+ cytotoxic lymphoma in adults; poor.
  • EBV+ T/NK proliferations of childhood: severe mosquito bite allergy, hydroa vacciniforme LPD, systemic chronic active EBV disease (symptoms >3 months with high EBV DNA and EBER+ T/NK cells), and systemic EBV+ T-cell lymphoma of childhood (fulminant with HLH).
