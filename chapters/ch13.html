Myeloproliferative neoplasms

CML, chronic neutrophilic and eosinophilic leukaemia, polycythaemia vera, essential thrombocythaemia, primary myelofibrosis, JMML and MPN-NOS, with the WHO 2022 criteria.

13.1General features

Clonal stem cell disorders with proliferation of one or more terminally differentiated myeloid lines (granulocytic, erythroid, megakaryocytic). Incidence peaks in the 5th to 7th decades.

  • Hypercellular marrow with effective maturation, so blood counts rise; no dysplasia.
  • Extramedullary haemopoiesis: splenomegaly and hepatomegaly; hypercatabolism (fever, sweats, weight loss, gout).
  • Progression: genetic evolution, growing organs, rising or falling counts, myelofibrosis, dysplasia, rising blasts, acute leukaemia.

13.2Chronic myeloid leukaemia, BCR::ABL1-positive

  • Philadelphia chromosome joins BCR (22q11) to ABL1 (9q34): a constitutively active tyrosine kinase driving JAK/STAT, PI3K/AKT and RAS/MEK.
  • Breakpoints: major (M-bcr) gives p210 (classic CML, e13a2/e14a2); minor (m-bcr) gives p190 (Ph+ ALL; CML with monocytosis); micro (µ-bcr) gives p230 (neutrophilic CML, thrombocytosis).
  • Phases (WHO 2022): chronic and blast. "Accelerated phase" is replaced by high-risk chronic phase.
  • Clinical: half are found incidentally; fatigue, sweats, weight loss, gout; splenomegaly in half.
  • Blood: WBC 12–1000 (median 80) × 10⁹/L with a "myelocyte bulge" and segmented neutrophils, basophilia, eosinophilia, blasts under 2%, platelets normal or high.
  • Low NAP score; high uric acid, LDH, B12 and transcobalamin I; pseudohyperkalaemia.
  • Marrow: very cellular, M:E ratio up, paratrabecular cuff of immature granulocytes 5–10 cells thick, dwarf megakaryocytes, pseudo-Gaucher cells; reticulin fibrosis in 30–40%.
  • Blast phase: blasts ≥ 20%, extramedullary blast proliferation, or any increase in lymphoblasts. About 70% myeloid, 30% lymphoid (mostly B).
  • High-risk features: high ELTS score, blasts 10–19%, basophils ≥ 20%, additional chromosome abnormalities (3q26.2, −7, i(17q), complex), small megakaryocyte clusters with fibrosis, resistance to TKIs.
  • Treatment: imatinib (complete cytogenetic response 70–90%, 5-year survival 80–95%); second and third generation TKIs (nilotinib, dasatinib, bosutinib, ponatinib for T315I); asciminib (STAMP inhibitor of the myristoyl pocket).

Monitoring response and residual disease

  • RT-qPCR for BCR::ABL1 on the International Scale (IS) every 3 months for 2 years, then every 3–6 months. Major molecular response is 0.1% IS or less; deep molecular response 0.01% or less. A stable deep response is a 4-log reduction held for at least a year.
  • FISH is reserved for atypical fusions.
  • Relapse: loss of haematological, cytogenetic or molecular response, or a 1-log rise in transcripts with loss of major molecular response.
  • ABL1 kinase domain mutation testing when early response is absent, response is lost, or the disease progresses.
  • Treatment: imatinib; second and third generation TKIs (nilotinib, dasatinib, bosutinib, ponatinib) work faster against most resistant mutations; asciminib binds the ABL myristoyl pocket (STAMP inhibitor). Untreated, chronic phase reaches blast phase in 3–5 years.

13.3Chronic neutrophilic leukaemia

  • Older adults (median 66). Splenomegaly, bleeding, gout.
  • WHO criteria: WBC ≥ 25 × 10⁹/L with neutrophils and bands ≥ 80%, precursors under 10%, blasts rare, monocytes under 10%; hypercellular marrow with normal maturation, blasts under 5%, no dysplasia; not CML, PV, ET or PMF; no PDGFRA/B, FGFR1 or PCM1::JAK2.
  • Plus CSF3R (T618I) mutation, or persistent neutrophilia over 3 months with splenomegaly and no reactive cause (exclude a plasma cell neoplasm making G-CSF).
  • Variable survival; ASXL1 worsens it.

13.5Polycythaemia vera

  • JAK2 V617F in over 95% (pseudokinase JH2 loses its brake), JAK2 exon 12 in about 3%.
  • Clinical: plethora, aquagenic pruritus, hyperviscosity (headache, dizziness, visual change), hypertension, erythromelalgia, gout, splenomegaly, peptic ulcer; thrombosis in about 20% including Budd–Chiari and splanchnic veins.
  • Laboratory: raised Hb, Hct and red cells (microcytic if iron-deficient), neutrophilia, thrombocytosis; low ESR; high NAP, B12 and uric acid; low EPO; low iron stores.
  • Marrow: age-adjusted hypercellularity with panmyelosis, pleomorphic mature megakaryocytes in loose clusters; absent iron.
  • Post-PV myelofibrosis in about 20%.
  • Treatment: venesection to Hct under 45%, low-dose aspirin, cytoreduction (hydroxycarbamide, interferon, ruxolitinib).
WHO 2022 criteria for PVAll three major criteria, or the first two plus the minor criterion.
Major: (1) Hb > 16.5 g/dL in men or > 16.0 in women, or Hct > 49% / > 48%; (2) marrow with age-adjusted hypercellularity and trilineage growth with pleomorphic mature megakaryocytes; (3) JAK2 V617F or exon 12 mutation.
Minor: subnormal serum erythropoietin.
The marrow is not required if Hb is > 18.5 / 16.5 g/dL or Hct > 55.5% / 49.5% with criterion 3 and the minor criterion.

13.6Essential thrombocythaemia

  • Drivers: JAK2 V617F (~55%), CALR exon 9 (~25%; mutant calreticulin activates MPL without thrombopoietin), MPL W515K/L (~3%); the rest "triple-negative".
  • More in women of reproductive age; half asymptomatic. Thrombosis (mainly arterial: TIA, digital ischaemia, erythromelalgia) and bleeding (acquired VWD with very high counts, aspirin).
  • Film: platelet anisocytosis with giant forms; no leucoerythroblastic picture. Platelet aggregation to adrenaline is absent.
  • Marrow: increased large, mature, hyperlobulated ("staghorn") megakaryocytes, little else.
  • Thrombosis risk: very low (≤ 60 y, no prior event, no JAK2), low (≤ 60, JAK2+), intermediate (> 60, JAK2−), high (prior thrombosis, or > 60 with JAK2).

13.7Primary myelofibrosis

  • Clonal megakaryocytes release TGF-β, PDGF and other cytokines that stimulate fibroblasts: reactive (polyclonal) fibrosis, osteosclerosis and extramedullary haemopoiesis.
  • Drivers as in ET: JAK2 (~60%), CALR (~25%), MPL (~8%).
  • Pre-fibrotic stage (30–50%): looks like ET in the blood; marrow is hypercellular with atypical clustered megakaryocytes (cloud-like, hyperchromatic nuclei) and reticulin ≤ grade 1.
  • Fibrotic stage: massive splenomegaly, anaemia, leucoerythroblastic film with teardrop cells; dry tap; reticulin or collagen fibrosis grade 2–3 with osteosclerosis and dilated sinuses.
WHO 2022 criteria for PMFAll three major criteria and at least one minor.
Major: (1) megakaryocytic proliferation and atypia with reticulin fibrosis ≤ grade 1 plus increased cellularity (pre-fibrotic) or with fibrosis grade 2–3 (overt); (2) not CML, PV, ET, MDS or another myeloid neoplasm; (3) JAK2, CALR or MPL mutation, or another clonal marker, or no reactive fibrosis.
Minor (confirmed twice): anaemia, WBC ≥ 11 × 10⁹/L, palpable splenomegaly, raised LDH, leucoerythroblastosis.

13.8Chronic eosinophilic leukaemia, JMML and MPN-NOS

  • CEL (WHO 2022): eosinophils > 1.5 × 10⁹/L on two occasions at least 4 weeks apart, clonality, abnormal marrow morphology, and no other myeloid or lymphoid neoplasm (exclude PDGFRA/B, FGFR1, JAK2 fusions). Organ damage comes from eosinophil granule proteins (heart, lungs, skin, nerves).
  • CEL in more detail: older men; some carry JAK2 V617F, JAK2 exon 13, KIT D816V or STAT5B N642H mutations, with DNMT3A, ASXL1, TET2, EZH2, SRSF2, SETBP1 or CBL. Blood: eosinophils with sparse granules, vacuoles and abnormal lobes; anaemia in most and thrombocytopenia in half. Marrow: hypercellular with orderly eosinophil maturation, Charcot–Leyden crystals, often dysplastic small megakaryocytes, sometimes ring sideroblasts and fibrosis. Eosinophils express CD23, CD25 and CD69; exclude a clonal T-cell population. Median survival about 2 years; abnormal karyotype, atypical megakaryocytes, thrombocytopenia and fibrosis are adverse, and imatinib rarely helps.
  • Reactive eosinophilia (commonest): parasites, allergy and drugs, vasculitis, skin disease, lymphoma. Idiopathic HES is unexplained hypereosinophilia with tissue damage and no clone.
  • JMML: a RASopathy of early childhood. Monocytes ≥ 1 × 10⁹/L, blasts under 20%, splenomegaly, no BCR::ABL1 or KMT2A rearrangement, plus a RAS-pathway mutation (PTPN11, KRAS, NRAS, NF1, CBL) or other features (high HbF for age, circulating precursors, thrombocytopenia, GM-CSF hypersensitivity). Noonan-associated and germline CBL forms can regress.
  • MPN-NOS: clear MPN features (often a JAK2, CALR or MPL mutation) not meeting a specific entity: early cases, advanced fibrotic disease, or splanchnic vein thrombosis with a driver mutation.
