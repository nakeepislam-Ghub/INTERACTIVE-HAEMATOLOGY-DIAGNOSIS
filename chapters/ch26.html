Principles and gating

How a flow cytometer reads single cells with light, how a sample is prepared and controlled, how the CD45/side scatter plot sorts the cells of blood and marrow, and why flow cytometry sits at the centre of leukaemia and lymphoma diagnosis.

26.1What flow cytometry measures

Flow cytometry detects cellular antigens while cells move one by one in a fluid stream: flow (moving in a stream), cyto (cell), metry (measurement). Each cell crosses a laser beam, and detectors record the light it scatters and the fluorescence it emits.

  • Event: the unit of data, one particle or cell.
  • Dot plot: a two-parameter graph; each dot is one event.
  • Threshold: a lower signal limit that ignores stray light, dust, very small particles and debris.
SignalCollectedTells you about
Forward scatter (FSC)Along the laser axis, up to about 20°, by a photodiodeCell size
Side scatter (SSC)At about 90° to the beam, by a photomultiplier tubeInternal complexity: nuclear lobes and cytoplasmic granules
Fluorescence (FL1, FL2 …)At set wavelengths through filtersProtein expression: fluorochrome-labelled antibodies bound to surface or intracellular antigens

The light signal is converted into a digital value proportional to its intensity and plotted. The fluorescence intensity is proportional to the number of binding sites on the cell: more antigen, more fluorescence, higher on the scale.

26.2Inside the instrument

Fluidics: the flow chamber

  • The sample is injected under high pressure into a sheath fluid stream of lower pressure that moves faster. This hydrodynamic focusing lines the cells up in single file.
  • Coincident events: if two or more cells cross the laser together only one event is recorded, and data are lost. Running a sample too fast or too concentrated causes this.

Optics

  • Laser source excites the fluorochromes; mirrors direct the emitted light to detectors.
  • Detectors: photodiodes (forward scatter) and photomultiplier tubes (PMTs) for side scatter and the weaker fluorescence signals.
FilterWhat it does
Long passTransmits light above a cut-off wavelength (for example, 520 nm long pass passes >520 nm)
Short passTransmits light below a cut-off wavelength
Band passTransmits a narrow band (the band width) around a centre, for example 530/30
DichroicSet at an angle: passes chosen wavelengths forwards and reflects the rest at 90° to the next detector

Electronics and analysis

Photons striking the PMT or photodiode become a proportional number of electrons, giving a voltage pulse that returns to baseline as the cell leaves the beam. Analogue-to-digital converters (ADCs) turn each pulse into a number, so the computer can plot the proportion of cells that are positive and the intensity of their fluorescence.

26.3Sample preparation and controls

  • Fresh samples are required. Dead and dying cells show reduced antigen expression, non-specific antibody binding and increased background fluorescence.
  • Adjust the cell suspension to about 1 × 10⁷ cells/mL in cold (4 °C) PBS.
  • Antibody titration: use the manufacturer's concentration, or stain known positive cells at doubling dilutions (1:50, 1:100, 1:200 …) and choose the one that best separates positive from negative.
  • Isotype control: cells incubated with an irrelevant antibody of the same isotype and concentration show the non-specific binding. A cell is negative when its fluorescence matches this control.
  • Appropriate controls are run with every test.

26.4Gating: the CD45 / side scatter plot

Gating means drawing a region around the cells of interest and analysing only those. The first gate is almost always CD45 against side scatter, the "flow differential". CD45 is on every nucleated haemopoietic cell, so this plot also excludes debris and non-haemopoietic cells.

Sequential gating combines gates in order. For example, lymphocytes are gated on CD45/SSC; within that gate, CD2+CD20− T cells are selected on a CD2/CD20 plot; both gates are then applied to a CD2/CD8 plot to count CD2+CD8+ cells. Try the CD45/SSC explorer.

26.5Reading histograms and plots

  • Histogram: one parameter. The x-axis is signal intensity (channel number), the y-axis the number of events. It gives the percentage of positive cells and their fluorescence intensity. The negative region is set with the isotype control.
  • Mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of a population is linearly related to the mean number of antigens per cell. "Dim", "moderate" and "bright" describe it.
  • Dot (density) plot: two parameters; each dot is one cell, often coloured by density.
  • Contour plot: looks like a survey map; each line marks a change in the number of events.

When is a marker positive? Use a threshold of 20% of the gated cells, except MPO, CD79a, CD34 and TdT, which count as positive at 10%.

26.6Flow cytometry in haematological malignancy

Haematological malignancies are clonal, uncontrolled growths of haemopoietic cells. Diagnosis combines clinical findings, morphology, cytochemistry, immunophenotyping and genetics. Malignant cells stand out on flow by:

  • Clonality: a monoclonal phenotype, and light-chain restriction in B-cell neoplasms.
  • Aberrant antigen expression: abnormal intensity, asynchronous maturation, or markers of another lineage.
  • A disproportionate increase of one cell population.

Flow is not the method for counting marrow blasts: haemodilution and variable lysis of erythroid precursors distort the percentage. Count blasts on the aspirate smear.
