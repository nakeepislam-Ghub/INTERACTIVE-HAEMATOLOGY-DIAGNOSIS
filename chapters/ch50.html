F7.2Red cell indices

MCV = Hct ÷ RBC; MCH = Hb ÷ RBC; MCHC = Hb ÷ Hct. An MCHC above about 365 g/L is rarely real outside hereditary spherocytosis and should make you look for a cold agglutinin, lipaemia or another artefact first. The workups section has a decision tree for this.

F7.3Physiological variation

  • Newborn: haemoglobin 140–220 g/L and MCV 100–120 fL at birth; white cells up to about 26 × 109/L with neutrophilia and a few nucleated red cells in the first days.
  • Infancy: physiological nadir of haemoglobin at about 8 to 12 weeks in term infants (about 95–110 g/L), earlier and lower in preterm infants. Lymphocytes predominate from about 2 weeks until 4 to 5 years.
  • Pregnancy: plasma volume rises about 40% to 50% and red cell mass about 20% to 30%, so haemoglobin falls (anaemia is defined below 110 g/L). MCV rises slightly, neutrophils rise with a mild left shift, and gestational thrombocytopenia (usually above 70 × 109/L) is common late in pregnancy.
  • Ethnicity: people with the Duffy-null (ACKR1) genotype, common in people of African and Middle Eastern ancestry, often have neutrophil counts of 1.0–1.5 × 109/L that are normal for them.
  • Altitude, smoking and exercise: altitude and smoking raise haemoglobin; acute exercise and stress raise the neutrophil count by demargination.

F7.4Pre-analytical errors

EDTA (K2 or K3) is the anticoagulant for blood counts. Samples stored for more than about 24 hours at room temperature show red cell swelling (rising MCV and haematocrit), and films made after a few hours show storage artefacts such as crenated red cells and neutrophils with lobes that look separated. Clotted samples give falsely low platelets. These are tested in the case studies.

F7.5Key points

  • Hb: men 130–170, women 120–150 g/L; MCV 83–101 fL; platelets 150–410 × 10⁹/L
  • WBC 4.0–10.0; neutrophils 2.0–7.0; lymphocytes 1.0–3.0 × 10⁹/L
  • MCHC above about 365 g/L: suspect cold agglutinin or lipaemia first
  • Infant Hb nadir at 8–12 weeks; lymphocyte predominance until 4–5 years
  • Pregnancy: plasma volume up 40–50%, anaemia if Hb under 110 g/L
  • Duffy-null neutrophil counts of 1.0–1.5 × 10⁹/L can be normal
