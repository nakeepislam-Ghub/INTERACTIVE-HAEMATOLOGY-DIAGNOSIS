1.2Definition and morphological classification

Anaemia is a decreased red cell mass, which lowers the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood.

1.3Aetiological classification

  1. Deficiency of building materials: iron deficiency; vitamin B12 and folate deficiency (megaloblastic); protein-calorie malnutrition.
  2. Bone marrow disease: aplastic anaemia; infiltration by leukaemia, lymphoma, myeloma, fibrosis or granuloma.
  3. Blood loss: acute loss gives a normocytic (or macrocytic, from reticulocytes) anaemia; chronic loss gives a microcytic hypochromic anaemia through iron deficiency.
  4. Haemolysis.
  5. Dilution: pregnancy, splenomegaly.

1.4Hypoproliferative anaemia and the reticulocyte count

A hypoproliferative anaemia has marrow erythropoiesis and a reticulocyte count lower than expected for the degree of anaemia. Causes:

  • Low erythropoietin: chronic renal failure, endocrine disease, protein-calorie malnutrition, low haemoglobin oxygen affinity, inflammation.
  • Iron shortage: absolute (iron deficiency) or functional (anaemia of chronic disorders).
  • Marrow damage: chemotherapy, radiotherapy, infiltration.

1.5Normoblastaemia

Nucleated red cells in the peripheral blood. Causes and their clues:

  • Haemorrhage: history of blood loss, normocytic anaemia, reticulocytosis.
  • Haemolysis and haemolytic crisis: reticulocytosis, jaundice, high indirect bilirubin, low haptoglobin.
  • After splenectomy: transient neutrophilia and thrombocytosis, lasting lymphocytosis and monocytosis, anisopoikilocytosis.
  • Thalassaemia: family history, splenomegaly, transfusions, microcytic hypochromic anaemia; confirmed by electrophoresis, HPLC and genetics.
  • Marrow infiltration: leucoerythroblastic picture with myeloma, lymphoma, leukaemia, metastatic carcinoma, myelofibrosis or storage disease.
