Von Willebrand disease

The commonest inherited bleeding disorder: types, laboratory diagnosis, platelet-type VWD and acquired VWD.

Source: Dr Abeer's Hemostasis notes, pp 155–157Reading time about 15 min

32.1Definition and clinical picture

An inherited, clinically varied bleeding disorder caused by a deficiency or dysfunction of von Willebrand factor (VWF). VWF has two jobs, so the bleeding has two components:

  • Platelet adhesion in small, high-shear vessels: mucocutaneous bleeding such as easy bruising, epistaxis, gum bleeding, menorrhagia, bleeding after dental extraction.
  • Carrying factor VIII: when VWF is very low (type 3, 2N), FVIII falls too and deep bleeding appears.

It usually shows itself in the second or third decade. Family history is often unhelpful because penetrance is weak.

Key pointVWF and FVIII rise with stress, exercise, pregnancy, oestrogen, inflammation and in blood groups A and B. A single normal result does not exclude mild VWD; repeat the tests.

32.3Laboratory diagnosis

  1. Screening: platelet count (low in 2B), bleeding time or PFA-100 (prolonged except 2N), APTT (normal or prolonged).
  2. Specific tests: VWF antigen, VWF activity (ristocetin cofactor or GPIb-binding assay), FVIII:C.
  3. Activity : antigen ratio below 0.7 points to a type 2 variant.
  4. Further tests: RIPA with low-dose ristocetin (increased in 2B and platelet-type), multimer analysis, collagen-binding, FVIII-binding (2N), genetics.

Why VWD is hard to diagnose

  • The normal range is wide (VWF antigen and activity 50–200 IU/dL), and about 2.5% of healthy people fall below it.
  • VWF is an acute phase protein: stress, exercise, pregnancy, oestrogen and inflammation raise it; it is lower during menstruation and higher in neonates. Repeat the tests when in doubt.
  • Blood group O lowers VWF by about 30%, so compare with the same blood group.
  • Penetrance is variable, so family history may not help, and minor bleeding is common in everyone.

32.4Treatment

  • Desmopressin (DDAVP) releases VWF from Weibel–Palade bodies: first choice for type 1; trial first in type 2; avoid in 2B.
  • VWF-containing FVIII concentrates (or cryoprecipitate where nothing else exists) for type 3, severe bleeding and major surgery.
  • Tranexamic acid for mucosal bleeding and menorrhagia; hormonal treatment for heavy periods.

32.5Platelet-type and acquired VWD

Platelet-type (pseudo) VWD: a dominant gain-of-function mutation in GPIb makes platelets grab VWF, so both are consumed. Large platelets and thrombocytopenia. Patient's washed platelets + cryoprecipitate aggregate spontaneously; donor platelets + patient plasma do not. Treat with platelet concentrate.

Acquired VWD:

  • Antibodies that clear or block VWF: lymphoproliferative disease, paraproteins, MPN, autoimmune disease.
  • Reduced synthesis: hypothyroidism.
  • High shear destroys large multimers: severe aortic stenosis (Heyde syndrome), LVADs.
  • Adsorption onto tumour cells or onto many platelets (essential thrombocythaemia); hydroxyethyl starch.
