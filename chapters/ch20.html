B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma

The commonest childhood cancer: presentation, the lymphoblast and its look-alike the hematogone, immunophenotypic stages, and the WHO 2022 genetic types that decide treatment and outcome.

20.1The B-cell neoplasms in WHO 2022

  • Tumour-like lesions with B-cell predominance: reactive B-cell-rich proliferations that mimic lymphoma, IgG4-related disease, unicentric, idiopathic multicentric and HHV8-associated multicentric Castleman disease.
  • Precursor B-cell neoplasms: B-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma (this chapter).
  • Mature B-cell neoplasms: small lymphocytic proliferations and CLL, splenic lymphomas, lymphoplasmacytic, marginal zone, follicular, cutaneous follicle centre and mantle cell lymphoma, transformations, large B-cell and Burkitt lymphomas, HHV8- and immune-deficiency-associated disease, Hodgkin lymphoma and plasma cell neoplasms (Chapters 21–24).

20.2Definition, who gets it and why

Neoplasms of haemopoietic progenitors committed to the B lineage. B-ALL: more than 20% lymphoblasts in the marrow (blood involvement usual). B-LBL: a mass lesion effacing a node or organ with no or minimal marrow disease.

  • The commonest leukaemia of childhood: 75% of cases are under 6 years, with a smaller second peak in the 50s.
  • Risk: Down syndrome and other constitutional disorders; polymorphisms in GATA3, ARID5B, IKZF1, CEBPE, CDKN2A/B. True familial B-ALL is rare (germline PAX5, ETV6, TP53).
  • Without complete genetic testing the case is called B-ALL/LBL, not further classified (NFC).

Clinical: marrow failure, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, bone pain and arthralgia (a limping child), head and neck presentations in children.

20.3Laboratory diagnosis

  • Blood: anaemia, thrombocytopenia and/or neutropenia; WBC often high, but can be normal or low.
  • Lymphoblasts: from small cells with scant cytoplasm, condensed chromatin and indistinct nucleoli, to larger cells with blue-grey, sometimes vacuolated cytoplasm and several nucleoli. Coarse azurophilic granules in about 10%; hand-mirror pseudopods.
  • Hematogones (normal B precursors, prominent in children and in regenerating marrow) look similar: higher N:C ratio, uniformly smudged chromatin, no nucleoli. Only immunophenotyping separates them reliably.
  • Trephine: mid-marrow sheets of uniform blasts with fine chromatin; variable mitoses.
  • Cytochemistry: MPO negative; SBB faint grey if granules are present; NSE dot-like; PAS coarse block positivity.

Immunophenotype

  • B lineage: CD19, cCD79a, CD22 and PAX5. CD10 moderate to bright; TdT and CD34 usually positive; CD20 low or absent; CD45 dimmer than mature lymphocytes.
  • Myeloid antigens (CD13, CD33, CD66c, even MPO) may appear, especially with t(9;22), without making it MPAL unless lineage criteria are met (Chapter 18).
  • Stages: pro-B (CD19, cCD79a, cCD22, TdT), common B (CD10), pre-B (cytoplasmic μ), transitional pre-B (μ heavy chain without light chain). Genetics matter far more than stage.

Diagnostic criteria (WHO 2022): B-ALL needs more than 20% blasts with B-lineage markers (CD19, CD22, cCD79a and/or PAX5) and immaturity markers (TdT, CD34 or CD10), surface immunoglobulin negative; B-LBL needs an effaced node or organ infiltrated by the same blasts. Specific immunophenotypes and recurrent genetic changes are desirable. Germline testing is worth considering in familial cases.

Prognosis: good, better in children. Adverse: age ≥10 years, WBC ≥50 × 10⁹/L, slow early blast clearance, adverse genetics (e.g. hypodiploidy), MRD at the end of induction or consolidation, and CNS disease.

Measurable residual disease

The single most important prognostic factor; it guides treatment intensity and therapy before transplant.

  • Flow cytometry (sensitivity 0.01%): pitfalls are regenerating hematogones, hypocellular marrow, phenotypic shift, and loss of CD19 or CD22 after blinatumomab, inotuzumab or CAR-T therapy.
  • PCR of the clonal IG or TR gene rearrangement: a single clonal peak instead of a Gaussian spread of peaks.
  • NGS of IGH (VDJ and DJ), IGK and IGL rearrangements, every 3 months.
  • In BCR::ABL1-positive disease, follow BCR::ABL1 transcripts. The p190 transcript is typical of Ph+ ALL; p210 suggests lymphoid blast phase of CML.
