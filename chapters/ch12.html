12.1Myeloid neoplasms: the big picture

Clonal diseases of haematopoietic myeloid stem or progenitor cells, chronic and acute.

  • Genomic instability with stepwise mutations of proto-oncogenes and tumour suppressor genes gives uncontrolled proliferation, skipped cell cycle checkpoints, blocked differentiation and resistance to apoptosis.
  • Epigenetic change: altered promoter methylation silences or over-expresses genes, directly or through microRNAs.

WHO 5th edition classes

  1. Myeloid precursor lesions (clonal haematopoiesis)
  2. Myeloproliferative neoplasms (Chapter 13)
  3. Mastocytosis (Chapter 14)
  4. Myelodysplastic neoplasms (Chapter 15)
  5. Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasms (Chapter 16)
  6. Acute myeloid leukaemia (Chapter 17)
  7. Acute leukaemias of mixed or ambiguous lineage (Chapter 18)
  8. Secondary myeloid neoplasms, and myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia and tyrosine kinase gene fusions (Chapter 19)

12.2Clonal haematopoiesis

Clonal haematopoiesis is the outgrowth of a clone from a mutated stem or progenitor cell, whatever the cause. With age it is common and usually harmless.

  • Epidemiology: rare under 40; 10–40% of people over 65.
  • Risk factors: age (dominant), smoking, male sex, cytotoxic therapy, aplastic anaemia, germline variants.
  • Genes: mostly epigenetic modifiers DNMT3A, TET2, ASXL1, plus PPM1D, TP53, SF3B1, SRSF2, U2AF1.
  • CHIP outcome: progresses to a neoplasm in 0.5–1% a year; higher with large or multiple clones and with TP53, U2AF1, SRSF2, IDH1/2, SF3B1 or ASXL1. It also raises all-cause mortality, atherosclerotic heart disease and autoimmune disease.
  • CCUS outcome: risk rises with clone size, number of mutations, high-risk genes (TP53, PPM1D, JAK2, RUNX1, splicing genes, IDH) and the depth of the cytopenias. An isolated DNMT3A mutation carries a low risk.
  • ICUS (idiopathic cytopenia of undetermined significance): unexplained persistent cytopenia with no driver mutation.
Key pointAbout 30% of people with an unexplained cytopenia carry a mutation or chromosomal change. That alone is CCUS, not MDS: MDS needs morphological dysplasia, raised blasts or an MDS-defining genetic change.

Related entities

  • CMUS and CCMUS (ICC only): CHIP or CCUS with monocytes above 0.5 × 10⁹/L or above 10% are called clonal monocytosis, or clonal cytopenia with monocytosis, of undetermined significance. They can precede CMML (Chapter 16).
  • VEXAS syndrome (vacuoles, E1 enzyme, X-linked, autoinflammatory, somatic): a somatic UBA1 mutation in stem cells of older men. Relapsing chondritis, skin vasculitis, lung infiltrates, fever, macrocytic anaemia and cytopenias; vacuoles in myeloid and erythroid precursors. The ICC places VEXAS, aplastic anaemia with somatic mutations and PNH within CCUS.

Predicting progression

  • Clonal haematopoiesis risk score (CHRS): high risk (12.5 or more) 24% progress to a myeloid neoplasm in 10 years; intermediate (10–12) 2.7%; low 0.23%.
  • Clonal cytopenia risk score (CCRS) for CCUS: try the calculator.
  • The online MN-predict tool combines genotype, blood counts and biochemistry to estimate 15-year risk.

Management: low risk, clinical follow-up; high risk, regular CBC and marrow examination. For everyone with clonal haematopoiesis: assess cardiovascular risk (blood pressure, glucose, cholesterol), stop smoking, healthy diet and weight, and check bone health.
